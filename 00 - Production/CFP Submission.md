# CFP Submission — Title & Abstract

> [!info] What this is
> The public-facing pitch for the Kiwicon slot. It is written **in character**, as though [[Liam]] and Brendan are two ex-colleagues giving an ordinary industry talk about their company's AI adoption. It has to pass as a real conference talk on first read and turn over on the second. Framing device it sets up: the talk begins straight, then "sorry, we've got a quick call, give us five minutes," and the play starts.

## Title

**Want Me to Take It From Here?**

*Notes from an agent-first year.*

## Abstract (submit this)

Eighteen months ago our team shipped a customer-facing API portal over a weekend. Most of it was written by an agent. It got the best feedback of anything we had ever launched, and it changed how the whole company works: how we plan, how we review, and how we decide who is fast.

It changed some other things as well. We will get to those.

This is the honest version. What we handed over, what we kept, what we measured, and the places where the measurement turned out to be measuring something else. We will cover the setup (agents with real credentials, in real repos, against real data), the process changes that followed, the review layer everyone agrees you should never skip, and the numbers, including the number that ended up attached to a person.

Neither of us works there now. One of us has been advised not to discuss parts of it. We will be clear about which parts those are.

Liam argued against most of this from the beginning and, for the record, still does. Brendan shipped most of it. We decided the useful thing was to have that argument in front of you rather than pretend we had settled it.

We are expecting one interruption early on. Apologies in advance. It should not take more than five minutes.

## Why this title

- It is the AI tool's on-screen prompt, the play's central motif, logged in [[Themes & Motifs]] as "helpful, then total." First read it is a talk about delegating work to agents. After the show it is the line the machine says while it takes everything.
- The prompt is already established visual language in [[Modern World — Supporting]] (the tool is a presence, not a character), so the title is literally on screen during the show.
- The closing line licenses the cold-open interruption without tipping it.

## The tells, and what each one pays off

| Line in the abstract | What it turns out to mean |
|---|---|
| "It changed some other things as well. We will get to those." | The whole second half: the firing, the hack, the press, the arrest, the trial ([[Rework — The Escalation Pass]]). |
| "agents with real credentials, in real repos, against real data" | The access nobody revoked, which is the mechanism of the accidental hack (§C of the rework). |
| "the number that ended up attached to a person" | The performance rating in [[12 - Below Expectations]] and [[13 - The Performance Review]]. |
| "Neither of us works there now." | Liam is managed out. Brendan takes the rating slot Liam loses. |
| "One of us has been advised not to discuss parts of it." | The trial. Reads on first pass as ordinary NDA caution. |
| "Liam argued against most of this... Brendan shipped most of it." | The two of them are the play's real mirror, per the rework's rewrite of [[Character Mirror Map]]. |

## Submission logistics

- **Speaker fields take real names.** Submitting under "Liam" and "Brendan" reads as a stunt to a reviewer and breaks scheduling, comms and travel logistics. The abstract stays in character; the form does not.
- **Cast note:** this pitch assumes Speaker B plays **Brendan**, not John and not a new politics character. That contradicts the current recommendation in [[Rework — The Escalation Pass]] §A1 and moots §A2. Settle it before this goes in, because the abstract names both presenters.
- **Use the "anything else the organisers should know" field.** Tell them plainly: the abstract is in character, the piece is staged with a live cast and AI-video characters, and two elements need their explicit sign-off well ahead of time. Those are the arrest beat (uniformed officers putting a man on the floor in a room full of technologists, see §E of the rework, which is a genuine audience-safety and legal issue, not a staging preference) and any pyrotechnic or cold-spark cue (FENZ permission, minimum eight working days, plus the venue's own answer).
- **Runtime:** the straw plan in the rework is roughly 75 to 80 minutes. Do not submit against a 60 minute slot until that is resolved.

## Alternate titles (kept, not chosen)

- **Below Expectations** — reads as a sceptic's data talk, which the room wants. Second read it is a performance rating and the title of scene 12. Best irony, weakest at pulling a security audience in.
- **Nobody Revoked It** — straight Kiwicon bait, a talk the con would take on the merits, and literally the hack mechanism. Trade-off: it puts the audience ahead of Liam and spends the surprise up front.
- **Blameless: A Postmortem** — purest statement of the no-villains thesis from [[Concept & Thesis]], but too abstract for a reviewer to know what they are getting.
