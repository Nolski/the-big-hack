# John

> [!abstract] One-line
> A skilled handloom weaver and steady family man in the early-1810s English North, whose trade, town, and household are unmade by the power loom — and who is pulled, against his nature, toward breaking it.

**Mirror twin:** [[Liam]] — same fall (master craftsman → made redundant → betrayed → radicalised), opposite temperament. Where Liam is prickly and alone, John is warm, plain-spoken, and *embedded* in a family and a community. That makes his loss land in a different place: Liam loses his *self*; John loses his *people's bread*.

> Historical grounding in [[Luddites — History]]. John is a composite, not a real figure — but his world (croppers/weavers, the cottage trade, frame-breaking, the 1812 Frame Breaking Act, the magistrates and the militia) is real. Keep it honest.

## Snapshot
- **Age / station:** ~38. A handloom weaver in a Yorkshire/Nottinghamshire weaving village. Works at home; his craft supports a wife and children. A respected, ordinary man — chapel on Sunday, owed and owing favours up and down the lane.
- **Wants (external):** to keep his loom, feed his family, and pass the trade to his son as it was passed to him.
- **Needs (internal):** to remain a man who *provides* — his dignity is inseparable from his ability to keep his people fed. Take that and you take him.
- **Fatal flaw / blind spot:** **faith that fairness is owed to him** — that decency, hard work, and a fair day's labour will be answered in kind by masters and neighbours. The world stops keeping that bargain and he is slow, heartbreakingly slow, to believe it.
- **Voice:** plain, warm, concrete. Talks in things he can touch — bread, thread, weather, the boy's boots. Slips into the period idiom (cf. [[The Weavers — Pulled Passages]]). Not stupid — *unhurried*. Goes biblical and quiet when cornered.

## Biography
Born to the loom; his father's loom, his father's father's. The cottage weaver's life is hard but *his own* — he sets his hours, knows his worth in the cloth. He has standing in the village: a man others trust. He is not a firebrand. When younger men talk of breaking frames, his instinct is to counsel patience and petition, to trust that the masters and the law will see sense.

The power loom and the factory change the arithmetic he has lived by. The cloth he weaves by hand can't compete on price; the masters cut the rate, then cut it again; the factory offers his neighbours indoor work tending machines for a wage that won't feed a family but will feed *a* family *for now*. The bargain he built his life on is simply… withdrawn.

## Arc
Start: **the steady center** of his trade and lane (B1–B4). The loom/factory arrives (B4–B5). Neighbours take the factory work — survival, not malice, but it guts the cottage trade and him with it (B6). The masters cut the piece-rate (B7). He petitions, lawful and patient, and is answered with contempt and then the bailiff (B7–B8). Hunger in his own house; the thing he built his identity on — *I provide* — fails (B9). The frame-breakers, whom he once counselled against, name his ruin and offer him brotherhood and action (B10). He takes the oath and marches (B11–B12). We end on the brink.
