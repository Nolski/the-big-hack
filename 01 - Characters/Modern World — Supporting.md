# Modern World — Supporting Cast (AI Video)

Liam's world — which is now the only world. All of these are **AI-generated video characters** (see [[AI Video Production Tracker]]), with one exception: **Brendan is live** and has his own note, [[Brendan]]. Functions and pairings are in [[Character Mirror Map]].

## Kristina — the PM / manager *(velocity over craft; the smiling axe)*
- **Function:** the **same upbeat face across the whole fall** — she runs the standup early and owns Liam's review and exit later. In the opener she celebrates the just-launched developer portal (best feedback of any release) and thanks Brendan for grinding the weekend to ship it. **Not clueless** — when Liam attacks the API-key choice she owns it with real conversion data (keys convert better than OAuth), then steps in to shield Brendan; her usual deflection (smoothing friction into a *scheduling problem*) **fails for the first time** here, and Liam's credential jab leaves her speechless. Later she genuinely *likes* Liam, "advocates" for him in calibration, and **signs the severance anyway** — not a villain, a decent person executing a rational machine. This is where the betrayal is most *systemic*: the person who celebrated the launch is the person who manages him out, and means well at every step. Her instinct to reward raw velocity is the soil the layoff grows in.
- **Voice:** brisk, upbeat delivery-speak ("velocity," "quick wins," "activation," "let's take that offline") and modern-management euphemism ("I want to be transparent with you," "let's double-click on that," "this isn't a reflection of your value"). The kindness is real and useless.
- **Function in the pairing:** the smiling axe — see [[Character Mirror Map]].
- **Beats:** B2 (opener — celebrates the portal, thanks the weekend grind, owns the API-key call, left speechless; and the debrief where she first clocks the machine's reach and *warns herself not to* — [[07 - Heads Together]]); B5 (the win becomes the mandate — [[10 - The Win We Needed]]; the standup she can't control and the defense of Liam she wins and then privately abandons — [[11b - Best Practices]], [[11c - The Latest Model]]); B6 (calibration — she advocates, loses, and trades him for Brendan — [[12 - Below Expectations]]); B7 (the review — [[13 - The Performance Review]]); B8 (offboarding).

## THE CEO — the pressure from above *(not greed — fear)*
- **Function:** carries the company's survival. Genuinely relieved by the launch, genuinely hears the engineers' concerns, and genuinely can't afford them — turns "one good weekend" into the **agent-first mandate**, not from malice but from a bad dashboard and a hard boardroom. The decent man at the top of the machine; the pressure that rolls down through Kristina to Liam. Role-based (unnamed, like HR), but written warm and human so the steelman lands.
- **Voice:** warm, big-picture, founder-optimistic, fear underneath. "This is the one we needed." "I can't walk back in there and say some of the engineers have reservations."
- **Function in the pairing:** the pressure from above — see [[Character Mirror Map]]. The board is the thing he can't argue with, the way the market was the thing no mill owner would admit to having built.

## Marcus — the second lead *(and the play's whole relationship to history)*

> [!important] He is not a supporting face any more
> Marcus carries the Luddites. When John was cut ([[Rework — The Escalation Pass]] §A1), the history didn't go with him — it moved into the mouth of the engineer already on the team. Everything this entry used to say (deliberately ordinary, a face in the grid, a Tuesday) is wrong. He is the [[Character Mirror Map|mirror]] to [[Liam]] and second only to him in stage time across Movements I–II.

- **In one line:** Liam is obsessed with the technical; Marcus is obsessed with the political; both are exhausting, both are frequently right, and neither of them helps anyone. They are the same animal pointed in opposite directions, which is why they cannot stop arguing and why they both talk over Kristina to do it.
- **The register — get this exact or the whole thing collapses.** Politically correct in content, self-serving in performance. He is *right* — about the Luddites, about who captures the gains, about Liam's behaviour toward Kristina — and it lands as indignant, point-scoring, faintly pleased with itself. He defends women in rooms where it makes him look good and talks over the one in the room every single week. The play never has to disown him because the audience does it for us; that is the anti-mouthpiece protection and it is why folding the politics into an existing annoying man is safer than building a new character for it.
- **Never stupid, never a strawman.** The steelman rule in [[Writing Didactic Drama — Craft Notes]] applies to him too. The analysis is genuinely correct. It just comes out of a man nobody wants to sit next to.
- **Wrong about the details, right about the shape.** He confidently deploys the myths — more troops sent against the Luddites than Wellington had in the Peninsula, a real man called Ned Ludd, the Goose and the Common as an old rhyme — and Liam, pedantic and also correct, corrects him. The audience gets accurate history **through the correction**, nobody lectures, and [[Luddites — History]]'s myth ban is satisfied exactly as written: the myths are staged as things a man believes, not things the play asserts. It is also the funniest version, and it lets Liam win arguments he is going to lose in every way that matters.
- **Voice:** nasal, faux-procedural, constant volume, never quite rude enough to sanction. "Quick flag." "I want it noted that I flagged it." That bluster now reads as *political* procedure, which is funnier and more damning.
- **Plane: AI-video for now** (`AIV-055`), with a live casting option held open — so **write him plane-agnostic**. He can be argued with, quoted, filmed and heard; never touched, never handed anything, never blocking a doorway. If a beat only works with him physically present, it isn't the beat — find the version where his contribution arrives on a screen, which is funnier for this particular man anyway.
- **Arc: none. He is a fixed point** — identical in scene 2 and scene 20 while the world moves under him, until the unchanged position becomes unbearable. He never learns he wrote the sentence that ended Liam, and he should not.
- **Guard against him becoming the hero.** A Kiwicon audience will be tempted to adopt the one man who was right. Every time he is right, he should also be doing something small and unbearable. The specific tell to check every draft: **if a Marcus speech would survive being posted as a screenshot, cut it.**

### Where his arguments go (escalating)
- **Movement I** — first appearance, pure comedy, zero stakes: derails a standup with a tangent nobody wants. Plants "Luddite" as an insult he objects to being used loosely.
- **Movement I** — he calls out Liam's credential jab at Kristina, which is already written into [[01 - Cold Open — The Standup]]. He is right, indignant, and helps her not at all — she was handling it, and now it's about him. The whole register in about six lines.
- **Movement I/II** — the argument Liam wins. Liam corrects a myth, scores, and dismisses the frame entirely: *"they lost, by the way."* It comes back at the end.
- **Movement II** — after the agent-first mandate: who captures the gain, staged as a fight about one specific ticket, never about capitalism. Kill any line that could be a tweet.
- **Movement II** — after [[12 - Below Expectations]]: Marcus is the only person who says out loud that something has been *done* to Liam, and Liam rejects the framing, because accepting it would mean he was never safe. The diagnosis arrives and the patient refuses it. **Marcus does not know he wrote the number.**
- **Movement III/IV** — after the firing, he is the radicaliser: a colleague aiming Liam's anger, live, instead of an anonymous forum. He gets to be exhilarated by a situation he helped cause.
- **Movement V** — the material stops being a bit. The **Alien Enemies Act of 1798** was last invoked before 2025 in the **War of 1812**; the **Frame Breaking Act** is 1812. Marcus says the true thing and is treated as a crank. Verbatim statute keeps its line breaks.

Dial the `AIV-055` annoyance most of the way down for that scene; he is being a decent colleague, and that is the point. His "I want it noted that I flagged it" finally finds a place where things get noted, and it's a survey.

### Consider
- **He films the arrest**, and that footage is what the press cuts to mean the opposite. The sympathetic witness supplies the libel, which is exactly what this man would do instead of intervening. Works on either plane — the clip simply exists afterwards with his voice on it.
- **The press device and Marcus are the same argument:** the word "Luddite" was manufactured by newspapers after Mellor's hanging ([[Real Cases — Hacks, Trials & the Press]]). He has been complaining about that for an hour by the time it happens to Liam.
- **He turns the trial into theatre and Liam is mortified** — the Chicago Seven move performed by a man doing it for himself. Liam is a bad candidate for escalation; he's a literalist who goes quiet when hurt.

## HR / "People Partner" — the system
- **Function:** runs the offboarding. Pure process and euphemism: "transition," "impacted," "we're grateful for your contributions." The human face of an inhuman procedure. **First appearance is earlier than the exit** — they're in the calibration room at B6 ([[12 - Below Expectations]]), and in that scene they are the person most concerned with protecting Liam procedurally: won't break the peer feedback out by name, wants a documented plan with real criteria before anyone sits him down, doesn't want the first written thing in his file to be the last one. Same voice, opposite effect. "Non-regretted" is a required field they fill in without emphasis.
- **Voice:** scripted, gentle, immovable. Says "unfortunately" a lot.
- **Function in the pairing:** the system with a human face — see [[Character Mirror Map]]. The officers in Movement IV are the same register escalated one notch, and they are now the play's only bailiff.
- **Beats:** B6 ([[12 - Below Expectations]]), B8.

> [!note] The AI coding tool is not a character
> The tool that displaces Liam is a **force** the humans use and talk about, not a figure to cast or voice — its menace is in how it's *deployed*, not in any personality. It appears only as on-screen UI / screen captures (see [[AI Video Production Tracker]] and [[Staging — Two Speakers]]); anything that looks like it "talking" is written as stage direction, never dialogue. Keep its look consistent — a warm corporate-synthetic interface, streaming text, a default on-screen prompt of "Want me to take it from here?" — so the late-play menace is earned.
