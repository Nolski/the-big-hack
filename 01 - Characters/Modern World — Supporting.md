# Modern World — Supporting Cast (AI Video)

Liam's world. All of these are **AI-generated video characters** (see [[AI Video Production Tracker]]); each has a mirror in [[Historical World — Supporting]] — see [[Character Mirror Map]].

## Kristina — the PM / manager *(velocity over craft; the smiling axe)*
- **Function:** the **same upbeat face across the whole fall** — she runs the standup early and owns Liam's review and exit later. In the opener she celebrates the just-launched developer portal (best feedback of any release) and thanks Brendan for grinding the weekend to ship it. **Not clueless** — when Liam attacks the API-key choice she owns it with real conversion data (keys convert better than OAuth), then steps in to shield Brendan; her usual deflection (smoothing friction into a *scheduling problem*) **fails for the first time** here, and Liam's credential jab leaves her speechless. Later she genuinely *likes* Liam, "advocates" for him in calibration, and **signs the severance anyway** — not a villain, a decent person executing a rational machine. This is where the betrayal is most *systemic*: the person who celebrated the launch is the person who manages him out, and means well at every step. Her instinct to reward raw velocity is the soil the layoff grows in.
- **Voice:** brisk, upbeat delivery-speak ("velocity," "quick wins," "activation," "let's take that offline") and modern-management euphemism ("I want to be transparent with you," "let's double-click on that," "this isn't a reflection of your value"). The kindness is real and useless.
- **Mirror:** [[Historical World — Supporting|Mr. Cartwright]] — the smiling axe; she softens toward his "improvement" boosterism.
- **Beats:** B2 (opener — celebrates the portal, thanks the weekend grind, owns the API-key call, left speechless; and the debrief where she first clocks the machine's reach and *warns herself not to* — [[07 - Heads Together]]); B5 (the win becomes the mandate — [[10 - The Win We Needed]]; the standup she can't control and the defense of Liam she wins and then privately abandons — [[11b - Best Practices]], [[11c - The Latest Model]]); B6 (calibration — she advocates, loses, and trades him for Brendan — [[12 - Below Expectations]]); B7 (the review — [[13 - The Performance Review]]); B8 (offboarding).

## Brendan — the empathetic adopter *(the conflicted one)*
- **Function:** the **one** colleague who doesn't hate Liam. Recognises Liam is genuinely talented but a pain; finds the AI tools genuinely useful and keeps using them; is conflicted by Liam's pushback — **not conflicted enough to stop.** Built and **shipped** the developer-portal API by vibecoding it (Claude Code, Opus 4.8) — it launched to the best feedback of any product yet — and means it sincerely when he offers to review it together. In the opener he's the audience's foothold: the decent man who gets stepped on.
- **Voice:** open, modest, conciliatory — reaches to *collaborate* even mid-humiliation ("maybe we could go through it together?"). Gets cut off.
- **Mirror:** a softer blend of the [[Historical World — Supporting|fellow weavers]] who take the new work — earnest, useful-minded, ultimately complicit by inertia rather than malice.
- **The responsible adopter (not a corner-cutter).** He uses the tool *carefully* — in [[06 - Good Instinct]] he pastes only the handler code into Claude, never the prod session logs, because he actually heard Liam's one legitimate objection. That's the tragedy sharpened: he's not seduced by a shortcut, he's better at the new shape of the job. The first **flicker of guilt** he can't name lands here (he lets Liam credit "his instinct" for what the tool found) and grows into the silence of B6.
- **He also carries the passive betrayal (was Sam's).** The friend who goes quiet is now the same man as the friend who helps — see the cast note below. His B6 is not cowardice but **honesty**: asked a leading question by a feedback form, he writes four true paragraphs and, for the first time in the play, doesn't hold backspace ([[12 - Below Expectations]]). Afterwards he is awkward and avoidant with Liam, and he takes the rating slot Liam loses without ever knowing the two things are connected.
- **Beats:** B2 (opener; the day-two huddle where he quietly solves Liam's race condition with Claude and lets Liam thank *him* — [[06 - Good Instinct]]; and the debrief where he can't take the credit and, honest to a fault, half-exposes Liam to Kristina anyway — [[07 - Heads Together]]); B5 (the huddle at Liam's rig, and the follow-up 1:1 where he makes the case and folds — [[11b - Best Practices]], [[11c - The Latest Model]]); B6 (the feedback round — [[12 - Below Expectations]]); B8, B9 (avoidant; the messages left on read).

> [!note] Cast decision — Sam is cut; Brendan absorbs him
> **Resolved before drafting B6.** There is no separate "friend who goes quiet." Brendan carries both the sympathetic-adopter arc and the passive betrayal, because one conflicted man is harder to dismiss than two thin ones — and because the betrayal reads worse when the person doing it is the only one who ever tried to help. The pure "goes quiet" register survives without cast, as the four-second "no concerns" response in [[12 - Below Expectations]]. Sam's voice entry and locked vector stay in `storyboard/storyboard.yaml` unused; nothing depends on removing them.
> **Kristina is a single consolidated manager role** — one person is both the standup PM (B2) and the manager who runs the review and offboarding (B7–B8); the smiling axe is a single face. (Marcus is deliberately kept ordinary — see his entry — and carries no betrayer arc unless a scene earns it.)

## THE CEO — the pressure from above *(not greed — fear)*
- **Function:** carries the company's survival. Genuinely relieved by the launch, genuinely hears the engineers' concerns, and genuinely can't afford them — turns "one good weekend" into the **agent-first mandate**, not from malice but from a bad dashboard and a hard boardroom. The decent man at the top of the machine; the pressure that rolls down through Kristina to Liam. Role-based (unnamed, like HR), but written warm and human so the steelman lands.
- **Voice:** warm, big-picture, founder-optimistic, fear underneath. "This is the one we needed." "I can't walk back in there and say some of the engineers have reservations."
- **Mirror:** no single historical character — he's the modern face of the **market pressure** even [[Historical World — Supporting|Mr. Cartwright]] answers to ("Manchester sets the price"). The board ≈ the buyers who "read the number." Reconcile in [[Character Mirror Map]].
- **Beats:** B5 — the win becomes the mandate ([[10 - The Win We Needed]]).

## Marcus — another engineer on the team
- **Function:** deliberately ordinary — a software engineer who adopted the tools like everyone else and ships fast by accepting what the agent writes (Kristina's "three of those out the door yesterday"). What the show establishes about him is only what it needs: in [[11b - Best Practices]] he hears Liam name him, unmutes to defend himself, and turns out to be exactly stubborn enough to keep a pointless fight alive — Chinese models, poisoned weights, one-character backdoors, "I want it noted that I flagged it." He talks over Kristina, challenges Liam competently, resolves nothing, and stays just professional enough that there's nothing to sanction. Later he exists in the review only as "the Marcus PR" ([[13 - The Performance Review]]).
- **Voice:** in the standup moment: exceptionally annoying — nasal, condescending, faux-procedural ("quick flag," "I want it noted"), constant volume, never quite rude.
- **Mirror:** none required; keep him a face in the grid.
- **Not a betrayer, and this matters at B6.** In [[12 - Below Expectations]] he fills in the peer feedback form and encourages Brendan to do the same, warmly, because he sincerely thinks it's the only channel where anything gets fixed — and what he writes is factually true. Dial the `AIV-055` annoyance most of the way down for that scene; he is being a decent colleague. His "I want it noted that I flagged it" finally finds a place where things get noted, and it's a survey.
- **Beats:** B5 ([[11b - Best Practices]]); B6 ([[12 - Below Expectations]]); referenced in B7.

## HR / "People Partner" — the system
- **Function:** runs the offboarding. Pure process and euphemism: "transition," "impacted," "we're grateful for your contributions." The human face of an inhuman procedure. **First appearance is earlier than the exit** — they're in the calibration room at B6 ([[12 - Below Expectations]]), and in that scene they are the person most concerned with protecting Liam procedurally: won't break the peer feedback out by name, wants a documented plan with real criteria before anyone sits him down, doesn't want the first written thing in his file to be the last one. Same voice, opposite effect. "Non-regretted" is a required field they fill in without emphasis.
- **Voice:** scripted, gentle, immovable. Says "unfortunately" a lot.
- **Mirror:** [[Historical World — Supporting|The Magistrate]] / the bailiff.
- **Beats:** B6 ([[12 - Below Expectations]]), B8.

> [!note] The AI coding tool is not a character
> The tool that displaces Liam is a **force** the humans use and talk about, not a figure to cast or voice — its menace is in how it's *deployed*, not in any personality. It appears only as on-screen UI / screen captures (see [[AI Video Production Tracker]] and [[Staging — Two Speakers]]); anything that looks like it "talking" is written as stage direction, never dialogue. Keep its look consistent — a warm corporate-synthetic interface, streaming text, a default on-screen prompt of "Want me to take it from here?" — so the late-play menace is earned.
