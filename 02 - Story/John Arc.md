# John Arc

The historical throughline on its own. Pairs beat-for-beat with [[Liam Arc]] in [[Beat Sheet — Parallel Intercut]]. Character in [[John]]; history in [[Luddites — History]]; voice + passages in [[The Weavers — Pulled Passages]].

## The line in one breath
A decent man who believes *fairness is owed to honest work* watches the bargain he built his life on get quietly withdrawn — the machine, the wage cut, the law — until he cannot feed his family, and the brotherhood he once counselled against becomes the only door left.

## Want vs. need
- **Wants:** keep his loom, feed his family, pass the trade to his son.
- **Needs:** to remain a man who *provides* — his dignity is his usefulness to his household.
- **The lie he believes:** "If I work hard and keep faith, the masters and the law will be fair." The world stops honouring it and he is heartbreakingly slow to believe that.

## Beats
1. **B1 — At the loom.** Dawn, rhythm, mastery. The cloth is his; the joy is real.
2. **B2 — Standing.** The tavern/guild; his counsel is sought; he belongs, fully.
3. **B3 — The marvel.** Cartwright unveils the power loom. Applause. John is impressed, not yet afraid.
4. **B4 — The last good day.** He weaves finer than the machine. "Let them match *that*." (Hubris.)
5. **B5 — The factory.** Production moves to power looms; the rate for hand-cloth collapses.
6. **B6 — The lane goes quiet.** Neighbours take the factory work to survive; they can't meet his eye.
7. **B7 — The petition.** He does it *lawfully* — patient, dignified — and is answered with a deeper wage cut and contempt.
8. **B8 — The bailiff.** The 1812 Act; the magistrate; Cartwright's affable regret. The law closes ranks.
9. **B9 — The cold hearth.** Hunger in the house; Mary's grief turns to fury. *I provide* — broken.
10. **B10 — The oath.** Becker's tavern: "Better dead than begin the old life again." Brotherhood; a target.
11. **B11 — The march.** John takes the hammer and goes with the frame-breakers — what he once forbade.
12. **B12 — The brink.** Hand on the loom that replaced him, hammer raised. Collapses into Liam's image. Cut to black before the blow.

## Turn (the hinge)
**B9.** Not the wage cut, not the bailiff — the moment **Mary** and the children go hungry and his lawful patience is revealed as worthless. The provider fails; the man comes apart; the oath becomes thinkable.

## Keep honest
- John is **simple, not stupid** — slow to anger because he is *good*, which makes the turn tragic.
- The Luddites were **not anti-technology** — they were skilled people destroyed by *how* machines were deployed and by a state that protected property over people. Dramatise that, not a caricature.
- Use real texture from [[Luddites — History]] (croppers, cropping frames, the 1812 Frame Breaking Act, the Yorkshire/Nottinghamshire risings) but keep John a composite, not a real person.
