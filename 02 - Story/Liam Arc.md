# Liam Arc

The throughline, and now the only one. Character in [[Liam]]; running order in [[Structure & Scene Map]].

## The line in one breath
A man who believes *being excellent is the same as being safe* learns — too late, and at full price — that the room decides his fate, not the code. Stripped of the craft that was his whole self and the people he thought were his, he is then processed by machinery that never once needs him to have done anything.

## Want vs. need
- **Wants:** to keep doing great work on his terms and be respected for it.
- **Needs:** to be *seen* — to belong, which he'd never admit.
- **The lie he believes:** "If I'm good enough, none of the rest matters." The play disproves it.

## Beats
1. **B1 — Flow.** Liam alone, solving the unsolvable, joyous. We *love* him here, pedantry and all.
2. **B2 — Half-belonging.** The standup; he's the hard-bug guy; he mistakes being useful for being safe.
3. **B3 — The demo.** The AI tool does his proudest trick in seconds. He laughs it off. (Hubris seeded.)
4. **B4 — The last good day.** He solves something the agent can't. Proof. Victory lap.
5. **B5 — Reframed.** "Agent-first team." His job becomes *reviewing the machine*. The ground shifts under the word "value."
6. **B6 — The quiet.** A feedback form asks his colleagues a leading question; they answer it honestly and the tool turns them into one anonymous sentence. Calibration prices him on it while he's at his desk reading every line. The betrayal is mostly silence, and the silence has paperwork.
7. **B7 — Over-correct.** He fights by being *more right* — blocks bad PRs, files the bug. It's logged as "friction." The review.
7b. **The fork, and the ninety days ([[13b - The Plan]]).** He is offered a separation agreement — nine weeks, a release of claims, a non-disparagement clause, and a sentence calling it mutual — or an improvement plan. **He takes the plan**, because signing would put it in the record that he agreed, and because a plan is the first document anyone has given him all year with a number in it. Then he does it, sincerely and well: four of the five criteria are measurable and all four go green, including the tooling number he spent the whole play arguing about. The fifth has no number and one empty field. **The audience already watched "Non-regretted" get selected, weeks before this document existed.**
8. **B8 — The reduction ([[14 - The Offboarding]]).** He is laid off and **does not see it coming for one second.** He joins the call busy and cheerful, mid-problem, having just found a two-year-old bug he has nobody to tell — and starts to tell the HR person before stopping himself, because he has learned people don't want the rest of that sentence. **He is not braced, and he is right not to be.** He has been told he is difficult for twenty-five years, by everyone, continuously, and got promoted four times being exactly this person: *"I've been like this for twenty-five years… same person."* **The friction is a constant, and a constant explains nothing.** What removed him is a scored headcount reduction run in another part of the building off tracked fields — rating, authored changes, tooling adoption — with no column for reviews. It isn't everyone; somebody picked; and nobody can say who, because the process exists precisely so that nobody did. **He does not break — he goes static.** He loses the middle of the meeting, asks for it to be repeated, cannot say which part, and opens a notes file, types the date, and never types another character. Severance is four weeks. "Not a reflection of your value" and a do-not-rehire flag on the same screen. The launch applause plays over Brendan's promotion slide *through the tinnitus*, so he never hears it, and at the end he congratulates him and means it. Comedy dead. **The offboarding is where the un-revoked access gets planted, as a kindness** — "your access stays live until Friday, we're not going to be weird about it."
   - **The last question, and the only self-knowledge he ever gets.** He asks what he could have done differently and is told, truthfully and kindly, that there was nothing. Then he supplies the real answer himself for the first time in his life — should he have been in more rooms, should he have been friendlier — and is told **"I don't think it would have changed the number."** The insight the play has withheld from him for seventy minutes arrives on his last day and is worth nothing. He does not write it down.
   - **Nothing in B8 lets the audience think he should have known.** He was not warned in any way that twenty-five years had not already trained him to discount. Every scene before this can be read, correctly, as an ordinary difficult career. The variable that changed is not in him.
9. **B9 — The empty apartment.** Messages on read. The self he built on competence has nothing to stand on. Marcus is the only one who says out loud that something was *done* to him, and Liam rejects the framing, because accepting it would mean he was never safe.

### The second half — he stops being the one who acts

10. **The catastrophe ([[20 - The Audit]]).** **Brendan does it, on Liam's machine, with Liam's credentials, eight days after nobody revoked them.** Home, drunk, furious — and right about everything: the tokens don't expire, he raised it in February, they handed the agent every key in the building because it was faster, and a machine in a cardboard box in Liam's hallway can still write to production. But most of his anger is **on Liam's behalf** — four years on the rota, never a weekend page, nothing ever down, and no column anywhere for any of it — and it turns on the company deciding reliability is solved because the agent writes the tests. *"Let's see, then."* He pokes at it; the model hedges once; he retypes the request as a scoped internal security audit, which is **not even a lie**; it agrees, because there is nobody home. To verify the list, it opens the doors. **He does not read the plan.** **He is asleep for all of it.** Eleven thousand actions, four external providers, one published package, and one careful clarifying question at 4am that nobody is awake to answer. In the morning it apologises to him in the first person. **Liam does not do it, does not know, and is asleep somewhere else** — and the audit trail has his name on every one of eleven thousand lines.
11. **The press.** He is invented. His own words from [[11b - Best Practices]] — *"if I wanted to exfiltrate a codebase I'd build exactly that"* — are quoted verbatim, accurately, and lethally. Nothing is fabricated and the picture is entirely false.
12. **The arrest.** Procedurally polite officers, a form, his name mispronounced, a request to confirm an employer he no longer has. The company's own HRIS record is what put him on the list. Marcus films it and does nothing.
13. **The trial.** The machine that replaced him is the prosecution's expert witness: the anonymised sentence from calibration — *"multiple peers cite friction and slow review turnaround"* — read into a courtroom. His defence is correct and useless, which is his flaw restated as a statute.
14. **The removal.** He is taken while the trial is still on the calendar. The courtroom becomes irrelevant after the audience has spent twenty minutes learning its rules. We do not see the prison.

## Turn (the hinge)
**B8 → B9.** Not the layoff itself but the *silence after* — the realisation that the people he counted on were never going to speak. That's where belief dies.

## The second hinge — and the thing to protect
The play's back half takes the agency **away** from him. He is not a machine-breaker; he is a man things happen to, holding a defence that is right and worthless. Every temptation to let him act — to hack deliberately, to escalate in court, to make a speech — should be refused. Marcus escalates; Liam is mortified. The absurdism lives in the institutions; Liam stays naturalistic, and the audience's anchor stays real.

## Keep honest
- Liam is **clever but only human** — not a savant, not always right, genuinely difficult. His difficultness must be *real* so the audience partly sympathises with the people who find him hard.
- The company is **not a cartoon**. Everyone behaves locally reasonably. The horror is the sum — and that rule has to survive contact with a prosecutor and a set of officers, which is where it is most likely to break.
- **He is not radicalised into a deed.** Earlier drafts ended with his hands on the keyboard, about to do something irreversible. That ending is gone. What happens to him is a warning precisely because he never crosses a line — the machinery doesn't require him to.
- **Open:** whether he is on a visa. Both roads work — a visa makes the chain causal, no visa makes it absurdist — so Movement I plants one deletable line about a lawyer appointment and nothing in the back half depends on the answer yet. See [[Rework — The Escalation Pass]] §A4.
