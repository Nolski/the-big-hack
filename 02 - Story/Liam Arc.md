# Liam Arc

The modern throughline on its own. Pairs beat-for-beat with [[John Arc]] in [[Beat Sheet — Parallel Intercut]]. Character in [[Liam]].

## The line in one breath
A man who believes *being excellent is the same as being safe* learns — too late, and at full price — that the room decides his fate, not the code. Stripped of the craft that was his whole self and the people he thought were his, he is adopted by a community of grievance and crosses a line.

## Want vs. need
- **Wants:** to keep doing great work on his terms and be respected for it.
- **Needs:** to be *seen* — to belong, which he'd never admit.
- **The lie he believes:** "If I'm good enough, none of the rest matters." The play disproves it.

## Beats
1. **B1 — Flow.** Liam alone, solving the unsolvable, joyous. We *love* him here, pedantry and all.
2. **B2 — Half-belonging.** The standup; he's the hard-bug guy; he mistakes being useful for being safe.
3. **B3 — The demo.** THE AGENT does his proudest trick in seconds. He laughs it off. (Hubris seeded.)
4. **B4 — The last good day.** He solves something the agent can't. Proof. Victory lap.
5. **B5 — Reframed.** "Agent-first team." His job becomes *reviewing the machine*. The ground shifts under the word "value."
6. **B6 — The quiet.** Marcus recasts him as a "bottleneck"; Sam says nothing. The betrayal is mostly silence.
7. **B7 — Over-correct.** He fights by being *more right* — blocks bad PRs, files the bug. It's logged as "friction." The review.
8. **B8 — Managed out.** HR. "Not a reflection of your value." Kristina signs. The B3 applause replays. Comedy dead.
9. **B9 — The empty apartment.** Messages on read. The self he built on competence has nothing to stand on.
10. **B10 — The forum.** Strangers name his betrayal and aim his rage. Belonging, of the worst kind.
11. **B11 — The decision.** He chooses to act. (Keep the act abstract; the point is the crossed line.)
12. **B12 — The brink.** Hands on the keyboard. Collapses into John's image. Cut to black before the deed.

## Turn (the hinge)
**B8 → B9.** Not the layoff itself but the *silence after* — the realisation that the people he counted on were never going to speak. That's where belief dies and the search for new belonging begins.

## Keep honest
- Liam is **clever but only human** — not a savant, not always right, genuinely difficult. His difficultness must be *real* so the audience partly sympathises with the people who find him hard.
- The company is **not a cartoon**. Everyone behaves locally reasonably. The horror is the sum.
- His radicalisation is **a warning, not an endorsement** — earned, tragic, and not to be cheered.
