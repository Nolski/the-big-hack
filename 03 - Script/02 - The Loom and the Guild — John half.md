# Scene 02 — The Loom and the Guild — John half

> [!info] Beat
> Dramatizes **[[Beat Sheet — Parallel Intercut#B1 — At the height of the craft|B1 — At the height of the craft]]** and **[[Beat Sheet — Parallel Intercut#B2 — The community (where he has standing)|B2 — The community]]** — the John-world twin of the cold-open standup. The show **rewinds 200 years** here on a neutral narrator V.O. Mirror twin scene: **[[01 - Cold Open — The Standup]]** (same craft + community, opposite texture — Liam *tolerated and sharp*; John *embedded and warm*).

## Purpose
Open John's world warm and whole, and bank the sympathy the cold open withheld. Like the Liam standup, it's an ordinary working morning — **nobody explains anything**. John weaves; his wife and a neighbour speak from the screens around him; the world (the trade he'd pass to his son, his standing, the rumour of a steam mill, his easy faith that the law protects him) leaks out *sideways* through normal talk. Everything he's casually sure of here gets taken later.

## Setting / Staging
**John** (live, Speaker B) is the only physical figure on stage, at an ordinary **cottage handloom** — a timber frame with foot treadles and a hand-thrown (flying) shuttle. He's working a length of cloth ("a cut") and wants it done before the cloth-carrier comes round.

Everyone else is **AI video on the surrounding screens**, each in their own space — **Mary** in the kitchen window, the **young weaver** out in the lane. As in the cold-open Zoom, **the live actor and the video never share the stage plane and never physically interact** — no shared props, no touching, no one stepping into John's space. John speaks toward the screens and works on; the clips are operator-cued with breathing room so his live lines land in the gaps. A continuous morning, one stage picture. At the very end, the menace (the power loom) is heard, not seen — distant.

## AI Video Cues
- **[[AI Video Production Tracker|AIV-030]] — Mary, kitchen window (warm, NOT uncanny):** bread's out, tea's going cold, the boy's asking after his own loom; later, the carrier's coming up the lane. Ordinary and easy, no fear in it. Best lip-sync, warmest grade — she's the emotional engine of the arc.
- **[[AI Video Production Tracker|AIV-032]] — Young weaver, the lane (responsive):** stops at the gate with gossip about Cartwright's new steam-loom shed; pushes John on what it means. John waves it off. The un-radicalised seed of [[Historical World — Supporting|Becker]]. His coming to John for a read on the news *is* John's standing (B2) — no tavern crowd needed.
- **[[AI Video Production Tracker|AIV-002]] — THE LOOM (power loom), distant:** the very end only — a faint, flat mechanical knock down the valley under John's handloom. The *menace* character (mirror of THE AGENT); a sound, not a sight. **Not** John's own loom.

## Script

*(Keyboard-clack from the standup slows and deepens into the knock of a handloom. Cold light to warm.)*

*(Title card, projected:* **THE NORTH OF ENGLAND. THE SPRING OF 1812.** *)*

**NARRATOR** *(V.O.)*: Two hundred years before that standup, a similar story involving Liam's own blood. This story centered a man, John. Good with his hands, proud of it — and about to find out the world had stopped needing what he was good at. He didn't know that yet.

*(JOHN at his loom — the one solid figure on stage, working. Around him, the screens hold his world: a kitchen window, the lane. He works while they speak; he never crosses into them.)*

> [!screen] VIDEO — MARY · `AIV-030` *(kitchen window — warm)*
> Bread's ready, John. Your tea's going cold.

**JOHN** *(live, working)*: In a minute. I want to finish this piece before the carrier comes for it.

> [!screen] VIDEO — MARY · `AIV-030`
> And your son's asking again about having his own loom.

**JOHN** *(live)*: He can have one when he's learned the trade. Same as I did.

*(He settles back into the rhythm. On the lane screen, the YOUNG WEAVER stops at the gate.)*

> [!screen] VIDEO — YOUNG WEAVER · `AIV-032` *(out in the lane)*
> Did you hear about Cartwright? The new mill by the bridge — he's filling it with steam looms. One man can run a dozen of them.

**JOHN** *(live)*: So I'm told.

> [!screen] VIDEO — YOUNG WEAVER · `AIV-032`
> A dozen looms, John. That's twelve of us out of work.

**JOHN** *(live)*: A dozen looms making cheap, rough cloth. People still pay for good cloth. Let him build his mill.

> [!screen] VIDEO — YOUNG WEAVER · `AIV-032`
> And when they buy the cheap stuff anyway?

**JOHN** *(live)*: Then we do what we've always done. Sign a petition. It gets sorted out.

*(The weaver goes on down the lane. John keeps weaving. After a moment, MARY again.)*

> [!screen] VIDEO — MARY · `AIV-030`
> The carrier's coming up the lane.

**JOHN** *(live)*: Good. *(he lifts the cloth off the loom and looks it over, pleased)* Tell him it's finished — and it's good work.

*(He folds the cloth. Far off, under the knock of his loom, another sound starts — flat, mechanical, steady, more than one loom in step. It doesn't get louder. It just doesn't stop. John doesn't hear it. Light holds, then out.)*

## Notes
- **Live and video never interact physically.** Corrected from an earlier draft (Mary in John's doorway, John handing the cloth to the weaver to feel — both impossible). The rule, same as the cold-open Zoom: John is the only body on stage; the others are images on the screens around him, each in their own space. He speaks *toward* them and works; clips are operator-cued with breathing room. No shared props, no touching.
- **Styling matches the cold open.** Same Obsidian markup as [[01 - Cold Open — The Standup]]: video characters in `> [!screen] VIDEO — … · AIV-###` callouts, live lines as `**JOHN** *(live)*`, stage directions in italics. Language is kept plain; the styling is the house format.
- **Show, don't narrate.** John has no to-audience speeches. Everything comes out sideways: the boy wanting his own loom (heritable trade / apprenticeship), the carrier coming for the finished cloth (piece-work), the steam-mill gossip (the threat), John's flat "sign a petition, it gets sorted out" (his fatal faith in fair dealing). Nothing should sound like a thesis.
- **Sympathy load.** We cut to John straight after the abrasive cold open, so this is where the audience finds someone to like. Keep him warm and unbothered; the dread is all in the last sound, not in him.
- **The mirror to the cold open.** Liam: a camera-off black square dealing out cruelty to a half-listening "community." John: present, decent, a household and a lane that come to him. Same beat, opposite soul — shown, not stated.
- **Mary NOT uncanny.** `AIV-030` needs the best lip-sync and warmest grade; if she reads as a deepfake here, the later hunger lands as horror instead of grief.
- **Equipment is historically right.** A **cottage handloom** (timber frame, treadles, flying shuttle — standard by 1812), weaving machine-spun cotton yarn bought in from a spinning mill; children wound the bobbins. The 1790s–1810s handloom "golden age": spinning was mechanised first, so weavers were the bottleneck and well paid. The **power loom** (Cartwright 1785) existed but wasn't dominant until the 1820s — here it's only a rumour. Don't dress John's loom as factory machinery; THE LOOM (the menace) is the *power loom*, heard distant at the end.
- **Deferred on purpose.** The systemic context (enclosure / the slave-cotton circuit) is better accrued across the John movement than dumped here — see [[Luddites — Period & Conditions]] §8. Land the slave-cotton beat somewhere it can stay incidental.
- **Standing without a tavern.** B2 is carried by the young weaver coming to John for his read, not an alehouse crowd. The alehouse clip is freed for **The Tavern Oath** (B10) — see [[AI Video Production Tracker]]. If standing feels thin, a second quick caller (a neighbour wanting a letter read or a docked wage looked at) is the cleanest add — left out for now to keep it tight.
- **Curdle seeds** (logged in [[Themes & Motifs]]): *"when he's learned the trade"* (the trade can't be passed on — apprenticeship clauses repealed 1814); *"sign a petition, it gets sorted out"* (the lawful petition fails, B7–B8); *"people still pay for good cloth"* (the finer-than-the-machine assumption; the rate collapses, B5–B7); the young weaver's worry, waved off (he becomes the [[Historical World — Supporting|Becker]] seed).
- **NEW thread to track — John as Liam's ancestor.** The narrator ties them by blood; that makes the Movement VI convergence literal kinship, not just thematic mirroring. If kept, thread through [[Character Mirror Map]] and [[Beat Sheet — Parallel Intercut]]. Flag for a story decision.
- **Period.** County kept soft ("The North") — John is a composite ([[John]]); "spring of 1812" is the historical peak. No myths staged as true.
- **Hand-off out.** The distant power-loom sound is THE LOOM's first appearance and the bridge into **The Demo → John half** (`AIV-021`). Carry it on the loom-clack motif per [[Staging — Two Speakers]] — no blackout.
