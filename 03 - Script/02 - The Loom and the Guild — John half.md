## Purpose
Open John's world warm and whole, and bank the sympathy the cold open withheld. Like the Liam standup, it's an ordinary working morning — **nobody explains anything**. John weaves; his wife and a neighbour speak from the screens around him; the world (the trade he'd pass to his son, his standing, the rumour of a steam mill, his easy faith that the law protects him) leaks out *sideways* through normal talk. Everything he's casually sure of here gets taken later.

## Setting / Staging
**John** (live, Speaker B) is the only physical figure on stage, at an ordinary **cottage handloom** — a timber frame with foot treadles and a hand-thrown (flying) shuttle. He's working a length of cloth ("a cut") and wants it done before the cloth-carrier comes round.

Everyone else is **AI video on the surrounding screens**, each in their own space — **Mary** in the kitchen window, the **young weaver** out in the lane. As in the cold-open Zoom, **the live actor and the video never share the stage plane and never physically interact** — no shared props, no touching, no one stepping into John's space. John speaks toward the screens and works on; the clips are operator-cued with breathing room so his live lines land in the gaps. A continuous morning, one stage picture. At the very end, the menace (the power loom) is heard, not seen — distant.

## Script

*(Keyboard-clack from the standup slows and deepens into the knock of a handloom. Cold light to warm.)*

*(Title card, projected:* **THE NORTH OF ENGLAND. THE SPRING OF 1812.** *)*

**NARRATOR** *(V.O.)*: Two hundred years before that standup, a similar story involving Liam's own blood. This story centered a man, John. Good with his hands, proud of it — and about to find out the world had stopped needing what he was good at. He didn't know that yet.

*(JOHN at his loom — the one solid figure on stage, working. Treadles knock under his feet, the flying shuttle cracks across and back, and the warp draws tight a row at a time. Around him, the screens hold his world: a kitchen window, the lane. He works while they speak; he never crosses into them.)*

> [!screen] VIDEO — MARY · `AIV-030` *(kitchen window — warm)*
> Bread's out the oven, John. Your tea's going cold.

**JOHN** *(live, working, throwing the shuttle)*: In a minute. I'll have this piece off the loom before the clothier's man comes round for it.

> [!screen] VIDEO — MARY · `AIV-030` *(fond)*
> Our lad was at me again this morning — wants to sit up at the loom with you and learn the throw.

**JOHN** *(live, a small smile, not stopping)*: He can sit with me after his lessons, then. He learns on mine first — hands have to know the weft before they're trusted with a warp of their own. Same as my father set me down. He'll have his own frame when he's earned it, and not a day before.

*(He settles back into the rhythm. MARY glances off down the lane.)*

> [!screen] VIDEO — MARY · `AIV-030`
> Young Becker's coming up the lane. He'll be wanting your ear again.

*(The YOUNG WEAVER stops at the gate, a length of cloth bundled under his arm.)*

> [!screen] VIDEO — YOUNG WEAVER · `AIV-032` *(out in the lane)*
> John — feel this. It came off one of Cartwright's new steam looms, the mill by the bridge. They're selling it in the market for next to nothing.

**JOHN** *(live, taking it in one hand without leaving his own loom, working it between finger and thumb)*: …Aye. I can tell from here. The weft's slack — feel how it gives, no body to it. Nobody's fulled it proper; the selvedge is fraying already. *(he holds his own piece up against it)* That's not cloth, lad. That's a rumour of cloth. Mine'll keep a man dry ten winters. This'll go through at the elbow by Christmas.

> [!screen] VIDEO — YOUNG WEAVER · `AIV-032`
> Maybe. But one man minds a dozen of those looms at once. Cheap as it is, they'll throw a hundred yards to your one.

**JOHN** *(live, back to his throw)*: Let them. A hundred yards of nothing is still nothing. Folk that can tell good cloth from bad will always come to a man who can make it — and them that can't will learn the difference the first wet week.

> [!screen] VIDEO — YOUNG WEAVER · `AIV-032`
> And when they can't afford to care which?

**JOHN** *(live, easy, certain)*: Then they'll buy it twice — once cheap, and then once right. There've been weavers in this valley since my grandfather's grandfather, lad. We're not undone by a bolt of loose calico.

*(The YOUNG WEAVER takes his cloth back, unconvinced, and goes on down the lane. John keeps weaving. After a moment, MARY again.)*

> [!screen] VIDEO — MARY · `AIV-030`
> The clothier's man is coming up the lane now.

**JOHN** *(live)*: Good. *(he unties the finished piece, lifts it off the beam and looks it over, pleased)* Tell him it's off the loom — and tell him it's good work.

*(He folds the cloth over his arm. Far off, under the knock of his handloom, another sound starts — flat, mechanical, steady, more than one loom in step. It doesn't get louder. It just doesn't stop. John doesn't hear it. Light holds, then out.)*

## Notes
- **Mirror to the cold open (the point of the rewrite):** John reading the bad cloth between finger and thumb — slack weft, frayed selvedge, "a rumour of cloth" — is the deliberate twin of Liam scrolling the merged PR. Both are master craftsmen who are *correct* about the shoddy fast-made thing and contemptuous of it; both mistake "I can see it's worse" for "therefore it cannot win." Keep the craft critique the spine of the scene; the jobs/market worry rides under it, not over it.
- **Period language (reviewer note):** worked in the equipment and idiom of the trade — handloom and **treadles**, the **flying shuttle** and the **throw**, **warp** and **weft**, **fulling** and the **selvedge**, a **piece** / a **bolt**, the **beam**, the **clothier's man**, an apprentice earning **his own frame**. Cartwright's cloth is loose **calico** off a **steam loom**.
- **The son beat (reviewer note):** it's *their* lad (Mary's line), he's asking to *learn on John's loom*, and John's answer is the warm, ordinary apprenticeship one — earn your own frame in time. No "he just now wants one out of nowhere."
- **Young weaver's entrance (reviewer note):** Mary names him coming up the lane before he arrives, so we travel from the household out to the gate instead of cutting to a stranger. He's **young Becker** — the un-radicalised seed of the frame-breaker he becomes.
- **The line that was cut (reviewer note):** John's old "sign a petition, it gets sorted out" is gone. His confidence here is craft-confidence, not faith in the law — that easy faith in the magistrate is set up and broken later, not here.
