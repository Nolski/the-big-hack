# Scene 13 — The Performance Review

> [!info] Beat
> Dramatizes **[[Beat Sheet — Parallel Intercut#B7 — He fights it the only way he knows (and it backfires)|B7 — He fights it the only way he knows]]**. Liam defends himself by being *more right* — and it's logged as "friction." Mirror twin scene: **[[16 - The Wage Cut]]** (John petitions Cartwright, same beat, same outcome). The curdle of the cold open: his pedantry from [[01 - Cold Open — The Standup]] comes back itemised as a behavioural problem.

## Purpose
Manage Liam out — and make the audience *agree with Kristina before they recoil.* Kristina is warm, honest, genuinely likes him, and **really did** advocate for him; her case (the work has changed; being right slowly isn't the job any more) is sincere and partly true. Liam fights the only way he knows — by proving he was correct — which is exactly the trait being recast as the liability. The horror is that no one in the scene is wrong and a good engineer is being discarded as a kindness. (Steelman per [[Writing Didactic Drama — Craft Notes]].)

## Setting / Staging
A 1:1. **Liam** (live, Speaker A) at a bare meeting-room laptop — and this time **his camera is on.** The cold-open mirror inversion: in [[01 - Cold Open — The Standup]] he hid behind a black square and was untouchable; here he shows the call his face, he's *trying*, and it changes nothing. **Kristina** is AI video on the shared screen, a performance doc open beside her — the two never share the plane or interact physically. Kristina is never cold; the kindness is real and useless.

## Script

*(It's time for manager lead performance review face to face conversations.)*

> [!screen] VIDEO — KRISTINA · `AIV-070` *(warm, straight)*
> Thanks for making time. I'm just going to be transparent with you, your technical bar is the highest on this team. That's not in question. Nobody writes code like you, and I've said that in every room I've been in.

**LIAM** *(live)*: …Okay. *(waiting for it)*

> [!screen] VIDEO — KRISTINA · `AIV-070`
> But the work changed this year. The part that used to be hard, agents do that now. So what we need from someone senior is less building it yourself and more moving it along. Unblocking people. And that's where signal from you has been tough.

**LIAM** *(live, careful)*: Tough how. Be specific, because I can be specific. If this is the Marcus PR, that had a race condition. It would have corrupted timestamps in prod, exactly like the thing we shipped in Q1, which I also flagged, and which...

> [!screen] VIDEO — KRISTINA · `AIV-070`
> You were right about the race condition. *(she means it)* You're almost always right. That's not the problem. *(a breath)* It sat for nine days, Liam. A four-line fix sat for nine days, and three people came to me, and the word they used was "friction."

**LIAM** *(live)*: I was *right*.

> [!screen] VIDEO — KRISTINA · `AIV-070`
> *(gently)* I know. I know you were. No one's debating you on that.

> [!screen] VIDEO — KRISTINA · `AIV-070`
> I've been in your corner all year. But I can't be the only voice in the room. I want to help you land somewhere constructive. None of this is a reflection of your value.

**LIAM** *(live, quiet)*: You said the agent does the hard part now. *(a small, terrible beat)* I'm the one who fixes it when the agent is confidently wrong. That's the job. That's the *whole* job.

> [!screen] VIDEO — KRISTINA · `AIV-070`
> *(a pause — the kindness, and the wall right behind it)* I hear you. Let's get time with your People Partner this week. Okay?

*(She's warm but firm. The summary doc sits on the screen.)*

## Notes
- **Steelman check.** Kristina concedes his excellence, concedes he was *right*, and genuinely advocated — and still it ends with him managed out. The audience should nod (he *did* sit on a four-line fix for nine days; the work *has* changed) before they recoil (they're discarding correctness itself and calling it care). If she ever sounds like a villain, dial her warmer. See [[Writing Didactic Drama — Craft Notes]].
- **His flaw is the trap.** Liam's lie — "being right is the same as being safe" ([[Liam Arc]]) — is exactly what the scene weaponises. He defends himself by being *more* correct, which is the behaviour being recast as friction. He never once addresses the room; he argues the code.
- **Curdle payoff (already logged in [[Themes & Motifs]]).** His cold-open pedantry and "nobody does this" contempt return here itemised as a "behavioural concern." The B3/cold-open applause for fast shipping is the same logic that now frames him as the bottleneck.
- **Camera-on inversion.** In [[01 - Cold Open — The Standup]] Liam never showed the call his face and was untouchable. Here he does, and it saves him from nothing — the quiet mirror.
- **The prologue — read before cutting it.** Calibration used to be the second movement of [[12 - Below Expectations]] and was moved here when that scene's back half became the phone call. It is doing three jobs that nothing else in the play does: it makes "I pushed back on this" verifiable, it is the only place the audience sees **Non-regretted** and the Exceeds trade, and it establishes that the deadline comes from above Kristina and the rating is hers to enter — which the To: line does silently, so no line of dialogue has to explain her remit. It runs about a minute, wordless, as a cold open rather than a mid-scene lull, which is the position that survives it. **If it drags, cut the deleted comment last** — everything else is replaceable and that beat is the scene's other half.
- **Where her numbers come from — and why one of them is wrong.** [[12 - Below Expectations]] stages the machinery behind this meeting. The nine days and the four lines are exact: someone pulled his review queue at calibration. "Three people came to me, and the word they used was 'friction'" is **knowingly inexact, and stays as written.** Nobody came to her. Colleagues filled in an anonymous peer form, and the tool generated the word in a summary line she couldn't break out by name even if she asked. She rounds it into something human because that's how she experienced it, and because "a model summarised your peers" is not a sentence you can say to a person you're managing out. Play the line with total sincerity; the audience who watched Scene 12 supplies the gap.
- **"Collaboration: Below Expectations" is a quotation.** The string on `AIV-071` is the cell selected in the calibration sheet in the prologue (`AIV-066`). Keep them identical. It is also the axis Liam cheerfully self-rated **Exceeds Expectations** on, twenty minutes of stage time earlier, in [[12 - Below Expectations]] — the collision is the payoff and neither Kristina nor Liam ever mentions it.
- **She deletes and Brendan doesn't.** The prologue and Scene 12's second movement are the same action with opposite outcomes: Brendan writes four true paragraphs and submits them; Kristina writes the one true paragraph that would explain Liam's adoption number and holds backspace until it's gone. The honest man's honesty convicts Liam; the advocate's caution loses him. Play both as ordinary, unremarkable choices made by tired people at the end of a day.
- **The trade — don't underline it.** One Exceeds, two names, and she picks Brendan for a completely defensible reason, alone, with a cursor, and nobody sees it happen. The man getting the slot is one of the peers in the summary and nothing in the document knows that. No music, no held shot. She does not mention it in the meeting and never will.
- **Mirror twin.** Beat-for-beat with [[16 - The Wage Cut]]: petition/defence made in good faith, met with a sincere reasonable "no." Keep Kristina and Cartwright tonally matched — warm, regretful, immovable.
- **No physical interaction.** Liam live; Kristina on the shared screen; operator-cued with breathing room.
