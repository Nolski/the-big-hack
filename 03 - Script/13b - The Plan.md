# Scene 13b — The Plan

> [!info] Beat
> The fork: sign and go quietly with money, or take the plan and fix it. He takes the plan, for the most Liam reason available — signing would mean it was true. Then he does the plan, sincerely and well, and every criterion with a number against it goes green. The audience already watched the classification field record **Non-regretted** in the prologue to [[13 - The Performance Review]], weeks before this document existed. Machinery from [[Performance Management — How It Actually Works]] §6.

## Purpose
Make the audience watch him choose, and choose wrong, for a reason they can't argue with. This is the scene that earns the offboarding: without it, [[14 - The Offboarding]] is a man being fired, and with it, it's a man being fired *after doing everything he was asked, measurably, at cost*. He is not in denial and he is not being stubborn — he is being handed a specification and building to it, which is the only thing he has ever known how to do and the thing everybody claims to want from him. Brendan comes closer here than anywhere else to telling him the truth and can't finish the sentence, which rhymes with the unfinished line before Submit in [[12 - Below Expectations]]. Nobody lies. The offer is genuinely generous. The plan is genuinely fair. The outcome was a required field weeks ago.

## Setting / Staging
Two desks, two pools of light, as ever — **Liam** live at his, **Brendan** live at his, one short call between them and no contact. The screen above carries documents only; there are no faces in this scene at all.

The back half is a **passage of time**, not a sequence of events: ninety days of a man doing his job well, told in checkboxes and a chart that comes down and stays down. Move briskly. The one thing to hold on is the empty field at the end.

## Live Cast

- **LIAM** — live, Speaker A. Reading, reasoning, and then relieved. Play the relief straight; it is the most painful thing in the scene and it must not be played as irony.
- **BRENDAN** — live, Speaker B. Two minutes of a man trying to say something and finding no sentence that survives being said.

> [!note] Live/video plane
> Both live men at their own desks on a call. No video characters appear; the People Partner is an email. Nothing here needs an operator cue except the screen states.

## Script

*(Two days after the review. LIAM at his desk. On the screen above, a new email — `AIV-072`.)*

*(From the People Partner. Subject: "Following up — options and next steps." Two attachments. **Performance Improvement Plan — 30/60/90.** And: **Separation Agreement and General Release.** The body is three lines long and the last one reads: "No pressure either way — take the week. Let me know by Friday which you'd like to proceed with.")*

*(He opens the second one first, because it's shorter.)*

*(Nine weeks' pay. Health cover through the end of the following month. Then the clauses: a general release of all claims, known and unknown. A non-disparagement provision. A sentence describing the separation as mutual.)*

*(He reads the word "mutual" twice.)*

*(He opens a call.)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: Have you seen one of these before?

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: One of what?

**LIAM** *(live)*: Separation agreement. *(beat)* They sent me one. It's nine weeks.

**BRENDAN** *(live, careful)*: …Right.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Nine is generous. Objectively. For a company this size nine is a real number — I looked it up, the median is four. *(beat)* Somebody argued for that.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: Are you going to take it?

**LIAM** *(live)*: There's a clause where I agree the separation is mutual.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: Yeah, that's — I think that's just standard.

**LIAM** *(live)*: It isn't mutual, though. *(beat)* It's one party's decision and a piece of paper that says otherwise. And if I sign it, then in every record that outlives this, it was mutual. That's what the record says forever. Nobody's ever going to open the file and find me disagreeing.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: …Yeah.

**LIAM** *(live)*: And there's a release. I give up any claim I've got, known or unknown. Which is fine — I don't have a claim, I'm not going to sue anybody, that's not — *(beat)* But you don't pay somebody nine weeks for a claim they haven't got.

*(BRENDAN doesn't say anything.)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: So either it's boilerplate, or somebody's already worked out what this looks like written down.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: What's the other attachment?

**LIAM** *(live)*: A plan. Thirty, sixty, ninety. *(beat)* Five criteria, dates against each one, check-in every fortnight. *(beat)* It's good, actually.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: …It's good?

**LIAM** *(live)*: It's the first document anyone's given me all year that has a number in it. *(beat)* All year it's been "the signal's been tough" and "friction" and "get on board," and I've been asking what that *means*, and now there's a page that says: median time to first review, under twenty-four hours. That I can do. That's a Tuesday.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: You're going to do the plan.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Obviously I'm going to do the plan. Somebody hands you a spec, you build to the spec. *(beat)* That's not a decision, that's just — that's the job.

**BRENDAN** *(live, and this is as close as he gets)*: Liam. *(beat)* The nine weeks is there now.

**LIAM** *(live)*: I know. I read it.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: No, I mean — *(beat)* It's there *now.*

**LIAM** *(live)*: Why wouldn't it be there in three months?

*(BRENDAN doesn't answer that either.)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: I'm not signing something that says I agree with them. *(beat)* If I'm wrong about the work, show me where and I'll fix it — I'll fix it in a week, I've never once not fixed something. And if I'm not wrong, I'm not paying nine weeks for a document that says I was. *(beat)* That's the whole thing. That's it.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: …Okay.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Thanks, though. For — yeah.

*(The call ends.)*

*(He replies to the email. One line: "I'd like to do the plan. Thank you for putting the criteria in writing." He means every word of it.)*

*(The plan opens — `AIV-073`. Five criteria, dates against each.)*

*(Median time to first review, under 24 hours. No open review older than 3 business days. Tooling adoption at or above team median. Attend and contribute to weekly planning. And, last: "Demonstrate improved collaboration and responsiveness to peer feedback.")*

*(Four of them have a number next to them.)*

*(Thirty days. The first box ticks. His review-latency chart — a graph nobody has ever opened — comes down and stays down. Anything under twenty lines goes out the same day, the way he told Brendan it would.)*

*(Sixty days. The second box. The third: his tooling cell moves off 3% and keeps climbing. He is using the corporate seats, on the corporate model, and he has stopped saying anything about it.)*

*(Ninety. The fourth box. He went to planning every week and he spoke at every one of them.)*

*(Every criterion with a number against it is green.)*

*(The fifth one has no number against it. Beside it, one field: "Manager assessment.")*

*(It is empty.)*

*(It stays empty for eleven days.)*

## Notes
- **The relief is the point, and it must be played straight.** When he says the plan is *good*, he means it — it is the first honest specification anyone has handed him all year, and his gratitude is real. If the actor plays it with any irony or foreknowledge the scene dies, because the audience's job is to know what he doesn't. They watched **Non-regretted** get selected in the prologue to [[13 - The Performance Review]]; they do not need help.
- **Both options are genuinely offered and the generous one is genuinely generous.** Nine weeks is above median and somebody had to argue for it. Keep the offer clean — the moment it reads as a trick, the scene becomes a villain scene and the play loses its argument. It is a good deal, offered kindly, and taking it would have been the right call, and he cannot take it without conceding the only thing he has.
- **His reasoning is correct at every step.** The separation is not mutual. You do not pay nine weeks for a claim that doesn't exist. He infers, accurately, that somebody has already modelled how this looks in writing — and the conclusion he draws from being right is the one that costs him everything. Same mechanism as the phone call in [[12 - Below Expectations]]: being right about the instrument does nothing.
- **Brendan's two unfinished attempts.** *"The nine weeks is there now"* and then *"It's there* now" are the same sentence twice, and he cannot get to the third version, which would be telling him. He also cannot answer "why wouldn't it be there in three months," because the honest answer is that he wrote four paragraphs in March. This rhymes deliberately with *"I want to be clear that I'm not—"*; play it the same way — a man stopping, with nothing stopping him.
- **The fifth criterion is the whole play in one line.** Four falsifiable targets and one judgement call, which is the two-axis trap from [[Performance Management — How It Actually Works]] §4 rendered as a form. Don't let anyone remark on it here. **Nobody ever remarks on it** — in [[14 - The Offboarding]] the plan is closed unassessed by a different process, and the field greys out still empty. The trap never even gets sprung; the man is removed by something that never opened the document.
- **The empty field held for eleven days** is the scene's last image and the only place to hold. It is not sinister and nobody is sitting on it maliciously — a manager is busy and a form is open. That is worse. **It stays empty forever**, which is the point of holding on it.
- **Everything he does here counts against him somewhere he cannot see.** The ninety days of reviewing that fixes criteria one and two lowers his authored-changes figure, and authored changes is a column on the reduction sheet in [[14 - The Offboarding]] while reviews are not. Play the passage of time as straightforward competence — **the scene must not know this** — but it is why the sequence has to stay in the show.
- **He stops arguing about the tooling.** The adoption cell climbing off 3% is him conceding the fight he was right about in [[11b - Best Practices]], quietly, without comment, because the plan asked him to. Nobody notices and nobody thanks him. It is the largest concession any character makes in the play and it takes four words of stage direction.
- **If it has to come down.** This scene exists to buy [[14 - The Offboarding]] its meaning; if the show has to lose time, cut *into* it rather than losing it — the fork can survive losing the median-severance line and the plan passage can lose sixty days.
