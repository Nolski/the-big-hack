# Scene 14 — The Offboarding

> [!info] Beat
> He is managed out, having done everything he was asked. The criterion he failed is scored against a survey that closed three months before the plan was written and was never run again — there was no available sequence of events in which he passed. Nobody in the scene is cruel, nobody lies, and the most decent person in the play tells him the truth when he asks for it, which is the worst thing that happens to him. **The un-revoked access gets planted here, as a kindness.** Machinery from [[Performance Management — How It Actually Works]] §6–7.

## Purpose
Land the blow, and land it on the audience rather than on Liam. He does not break, does not shout, and does not plead — he goes literal, which is what he does when he is hurt, and he takes notes throughout, including on how to be a person. The audience should be ahead of him the entire scene and unable to do anything about it.

The engine is arithmetic, not malice: the plan measured five things, he moved the four that could be measured, and the fifth was assessed against peer feedback collected before the plan existed. He works this out live, on a calendar, in front of a woman who agrees with him and cannot help. **The nine weeks he turned down in [[13b - The Plan]] is gone**, and he finds that out in the same conversation, and says "right, no, that makes sense."

Two things must survive: the People Partner is genuinely kind and genuinely useful, and the last thing Liam does is go back to work.

## Setting / Staging
A meeting room, and for once Liam is the only body on stage. **He is live, camera on, at a laptop** — the same exposure as [[13 - The Performance Review]], with nobody in the room to see it. Beside him, open, a document he prepared for this meeting and was not asked for. On the screen, the **People Partner**, whose face the audience has never seen before and will not see again.

The room is the smallest and barest space in the play. No desk clutter, no second monitor until the end, nothing of his in it.

**Kristina is not in this scene and her absence is load-bearing.** She delivered the review; she is not here for the consequence. Liam asks about it once and accepts the answer.

## Live Cast

## Script

*(Day ninety-eight. The plan, still open — `AIV-073`. Four criteria green.)*

*(The fifth field fills in. **Not met.** Under it, one line of rationale, and it is not unkind: "Improvement is noted and appreciated. Concerns raised in peer feedback have not been resolved.")*

*(The peer feedback round closed in March. There has not been another one.)*

*(A meeting room. LIAM live at a laptop, camera on. Beside him, open, a document he made for this meeting. On the screen, the PEOPLE PARTNER.)*

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074` *(warm, and she has done this a lot)*
> Hi Liam. I'm Dana — we haven't met properly, I've been supporting Kristina's team since March. Thanks for making the time.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Is Kristina joining?

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074` *(she doesn't dress it up)*
> She's not. For these it's usually just the two of us. I know that isn't what you'd expect.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Okay.

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074`
> I want to be transparent with you about why we're meeting. The improvement plan closed on Friday, and the outcome is that we're going to be ending your employment. Your last day will be the thirty-first. *(beat)* I'm going to go through the practical side properly, and you can stop me anywhere.

*(A pause. He does not argue. He turns the document round.)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: Can I show you something first? It's one page.

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074`
> Of course.

**LIAM** *(live)*: There were five criteria. Four of them had numbers. *(beat)* Median time to first review: target was twenty-four hours, I finished at six. Open reviews older than three days: target was zero, it's been zero since day nineteen. Tooling adoption at or above team median — median's sixty-one, I'm at seventy-four. *(beat)* Planning attendance was every week. I went every week. I spoke at every one.

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074` *(and she means it)*
> I've read it. It's a genuinely strong ninety days and I want to acknowledge that properly.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Then what's the fifth one.

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074` *(a pause)*
> Demonstrate improved collaboration and responsiveness to peer feedback.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Which peer feedback.

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074`
> The half-year round.

**LIAM** *(live)*: *(he opens a calendar — not fast, not triumphant; the way he'd open a log)* That closed on the fourteenth of March. *(beat)* The plan starts in June. *(beat)* So the thing I was being measured on for ninety days is a survey that shut three months before anybody wrote the plan, and there hasn't been another one since.

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074`
> There hasn't, no. The next round is November.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Then there's no version of the last ninety days where that line goes green. *(beat)* It isn't a criterion. There's nothing I could have done. Not — I don't mean I tried and it wasn't enough. I mean there is no sequence of events.

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074` *(the kindest and the worst thing she says)*
> I hear you. And I don't think that's an unfair reading of it. *(beat)* Unfortunately the assessment on that line is the manager's, and it's been made.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Can I talk to Kristina.

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074`
> You can absolutely reach out to her. *(beat)* It won't change the outcome, and I'd rather say that now than have you spend the week hoping.

*(A long beat. Then he opens a notes file, and types the date at the top of it.)*

**LIAM** *(live, flat)*: Okay. *(beat)* When does health cover end.

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074`
> End of the month following your last day.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Unvested equity.

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074`
> Unfortunately unvested is forfeit on the last day. Vested is yours — you'll have ninety days to exercise.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Ninety days from the last day, or from today.

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074`
> From the last day.

**LIAM** *(live, typing it)*: Okay. *(beat)* The agreement you sent in June. The separation one. Is that still—

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074` *(gently)*
> That offer was tied to that date. Unfortunately it isn't available now.

*(A beat.)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: Right. *(beat)* No, that makes sense.

*(He types that down too.)*

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074`
> IT will send you a returns label. It's the laptop, the monitor, the keyboard, and any dongles or adapters.

**LIAM** *(live)*: There's a USB-C to ethernet adapter that isn't on the asset register. I expensed it, it got rejected, so I paid for it myself.

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074` *(a small beat — she is not being funny)*
> Then that one's yours.

**LIAM** *(live)*: I'll put it in the box anyway. It's easier than explaining it to whoever opens it.

*(He writes "dongle" on the list.)*

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074`
> One more thing, and then I'll let you go. Your access stays live until Friday. We don't do the same-day lockout here — people have handover, and there are things they want to save. *(beat)* We're not going to be weird about it.

**LIAM** *(live)*: …Thank you.

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074`
> And I want to say — I know it sounds like a line, and it isn't — none of this is a reflection of your value as an engineer. Kristina has been very clear with me about the quality of your work. *(beat)* We'd love to stay in touch.

*(On a screen nobody in the meeting can see — `AIV-075` — the HRIS record. A field marked "Eligible for rehire" changes from Yes to No. It is a dropdown with two options. It is the same click as the last one.)*

*(A pause. It is the only place all scene where he stops before speaking.)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: Can I ask you one thing? *(beat)* Not to argue. I'm not going to argue. *(beat)* What could I have done differently.

*(She doesn't answer straight away. She is a decent person, and she does have an answer, which is worse than not having one.)*

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074` *(carefully, and meaning all of it)*
> Honestly? I've thought about it. *(beat)* I don't think the work was ever the issue, and I think you know that. *(beat)* I think you needed people saying your name in rooms you weren't in. And I don't think that was ever something you were willing to do.

*(A beat. Then he types it.)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: Sorry — "saying my name in rooms." *(beat)* Do you mean I should have been more visible, or do you mean I should have been friendlier.

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074` *(a beat)*
> …I think I mean both.

**LIAM** *(live, typing)*: Okay.

> [!screen] VIDEO — PEOPLE PARTNER · `AIV-074`
> Take the rest of today. Genuinely — log off, go outside. *(beat)* And if you want to put me down as a reference, I'll take the call. That isn't a standard offer.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Thanks. *(beat)* Thank you, Dana.

*(The call ends. He is alone in a bare room with a document he prepared, which nobody asked for, and every number in which is true.)*

*(Somewhere else in the building — `AIV-075` — an all-hands. The applause clip from the launch, the same one, over a slide: Q3 promotions. BRENDAN's name is on it.)*

*(On LIAM's second monitor a Slack notification: #general. "Huge congrats to Brendan on the step up!" Forty-one reactions.)*

*(He looks at it. He types "congrats!!" and a party emoji, and sends it, and he means it.)*

*(He closes the document he prepared. He does not delete it.)*

*(Then he opens his review queue. There are six things in it, and he has until Friday.)*

*(He starts reading.)*

## Notes
- **He does not break, and every draft will want him to.** Refuse it. `Liam.md` says he goes quiet and literal when hurt; here that is the entire performance. He asks about health cover, equity, exercise windows and a dongle, and he writes the answers down. The audience supplies everything he doesn't. **The only pause he takes all scene is before "what could I have done differently," and that is the whole break.**
- **The arithmetic is the truck.** Four falsifiable criteria moved; the fifth was assessed against a survey that closed in March and does not run again until November. He works it out live, on a calendar, and being right about it changes nothing — the third time the play has run that move (the phone call in [[12 - Below Expectations]], the fork in [[13b - The Plan]], here) and the last time it will be available.
- **Dana is not a villain and is the most useful person in the play.** She confirms the reading is fair, refuses to let him spend a week hoping, tells him the truth when he asks for it, and offers a reference she doesn't have to offer. **Every one of those is a kindness and every one of them is also the process running correctly.** Play her warm and unhurried. If a draft gives her a beat of smugness, coldness or relief, cut it — the horror is that she is good at a job that should not exist in this shape. Per [[Performance Management — How It Actually Works]]: "keep HR unvillainous."
- **The answer she gives him is true.** "People saying your name in rooms you weren't in" is an accurate description of how senior engineers are evaluated (§"What's different for a senior engineer") and it is also an unanswerable thing to tell a man on his last day. He does the most devastating available thing with it: he asks a clarifying question and takes a note. **He is taking notes on how to be a person.** Do not let the actor colour this — flat and useful, as if debugging.
- **The nine weeks is gone, and he says "that makes sense."** This is the payoff of the fork and it is deliberately underplayed — one exchange, no reaction, straight on to the returns label. Brendan warned him twice in [[13b - The Plan]] and could not finish either sentence. Nobody in this scene knows that happened.
- **The kindness that destroys him.** *"Your access stays live until Friday… we're not going to be weird about it"* is the offboarding's warmest moment and it is the mechanism of the hack. It is planted here and paid off in Movement III. **Do not flag it, do not hold on it, and do not let the lighting notice.** Logged in [[Themes & Motifs]].
- **The rehire flag is the "Non-regretted" rhyme.** A two-option dropdown, changed by nobody visible, while a person is being told none of this reflects their value. Same staging as the classification field in the prologue to [[13 - The Performance Review]]; same rule — no music, nobody notices.
- **"We'd love to stay in touch"** sits directly against that dropdown. From the euphemism glossary: it means there is a do-not-rehire flag on the record. She is not being sarcastic; she does not know. Both facts are on the screen at the same time and neither character can see the other one.
- **Brendan's promotion is the applause curdle.** The same clip that played for the agent launch in [[10 - The Win We Needed]] plays over the slide with his name on it. He is not in this scene and does not message Liam. **Liam congratulates him and means it** — do not let this play as bitter, and do not give Brendan a reply. Logged in [[Themes & Motifs]].
- **Kristina's absence.** She delivered the review and is not here. Liam asks once, is told plainly, and accepts it. Don't explain it and don't have Dana apologise for it; it is normal practice and that is the point.
- **He goes back to work.** The last image is the seed, not the payoff — six things in the queue and access until Friday. [[19 - The Empty Apartment]] does this in full: the work was the self, and nobody has told the self. It is also, exactly and unfortunately, how the hack happens.
- **Strings that must not drift.** *"We're not going to be weird about it"* is quoted in [[Liam Arc]] and is the access plant. *"Not a reflection of your value"* rhymes with `AIV-070` in [[13 - The Performance Review]] and should stay near-identical. The five plan criteria must match [[13b - The Plan]] word for word.
- **Runtime.** ~6 min spoken, the longest scene in Movement III. If it has to come down, the cuts in order are: the equity exercise-window exchange, then the all-hands slide (the Slack notification alone carries it). **Do not cut the calendar, the dongle, or the last question.**
