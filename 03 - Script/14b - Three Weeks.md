# Scene 14b — Three Weeks

> [!info] Beat
> Five days after Liam, Brendan gets the identical call from the identical person reading the identical page. He is the company's best performer, he was promoted the previous Thursday, and none of that is a field. He does what Liam could not: he argues, loudly, with everything he has — and it moves nothing, because there is nothing in the conversation for an argument to move. He ends it by apologising to her. Same script as [[14 - The Offboarding]], same deflections, same policy, different man.

## Purpose
**The script is the point.** The audience has just heard this conversation. Hearing it again, word for word, at a different desk, on a man with the opposite case, is what turns a sad scene into a machine — you cannot argue with a page, and the page does not care which of you is reading it.

**Brendan's case is genuinely excellent and it is the wrong shape.** Liam's worth was invisible because prevented outages leave no artefact. Brendan's is invisible because he asked his manager whether he should use a better tracker and was told yes. Neither of them is being judged badly; neither of them is being judged at all. **The scene must not read as "he deserved it less than Liam did"** — the reduction has no opinion about either of them, and the audience should leave with the sheet, not with a ranking.

**This is the only time in the play Brendan speaks up.** He declines to speak eight times across the show, every time on someone else's behalf; here, for himself, he says everything, at volume, and it does nothing. **Nobody in the scene notices this and no line refers to it.** It is also why he is flat and winded in [[19 - Nine Tickets]] — he has already spent it, and he is embarrassed about it.

> [!warning] The two things that keep him inside the character
> [[Brendan]]'s hard rule is **never insufferable, never witty at someone's cost.** Anger is new for him and it must stay *panicky* rather than superior. He never sneers at Dana, never gets a clever line off, never says anything about anybody else on the team — **Liam's name is not in this scene, and neither is Marcus's.**
>
> And **he never lands a speech.** Every attempt to say what he is worth comes apart in the middle, the same way Liam's does, and the one time he gets near the thesis he hears himself say it and stops. **If a draft gives him a good paragraph about the human element, it is wrong** — the line has to be embarrassing, not stirring.

## Setting / Staging
**A flat, not an office.** Brendan live, camera on, at a laptop on a small desk, alone, in a room about the size of the one Liam was processed in. On the laptop screen, **Dana** — same framing, same lighting, same chair, same everything — and on a second monitor beside it, a chat thread nobody looks at. She has not changed in five days and she will not change during this.

**No ring.** [[14 - The Offboarding]]'s tinnitus beat is spent and does not come back. **Brendan hears every single word of this meeting and it does not help him** — that is the contrast, and it is the whole staging note. No tone, no muffle, no light change, no music anywhere in the scene.

## Live Cast

- **BRENDAN** — live, Speaker B. **Cheerful and slightly sheepish at the top** — he thinks the call is admin about his promotion. Then confused, then insistent, then loud. **The anger arrives through confusion, not contempt**: he genuinely believes for two minutes that this is a records error and he can help her find it. When that runs out he panics, and when the panic runs out he apologises. **He must never get a good line in.** Every sentence that is going somewhere stops halfway.

## Notes
- **The script must be verbatim and the delivery must be identical.** The read-out, "I know that's a lot to take in… there's a pack," "scored against a consistent set of criteria… so it isn't any one person making a call about an individual," "that's not something I'm able to share, I'm afraid," "I hear you," "I know that's frustrating," "this is the one I can speak to," the returns list, the access window, the outplacement line, "none of this is a reflection of your value or your contribution," and the closing apology are **word for word what Dana says in [[14 - The Offboarding]]**, and `AIV-096` must be performed at the same speed and with the same emphasis as `AIV-074`. **This is the scene's entire argument and it is carried by the acting, not by a line.** If a rewrite touches any of those strings, it has to touch both scenes. Cross-checked against the strings list at the bottom of [[14 - The Offboarding]].
- **He argues and Liam didn't, and it makes no difference.** That is the point of running the same page twice. Liam went static, apologised four times, and lost the middle of the meeting; Brendan shouts, presents evidence, asks for an appeal and gets all the way to the top of his voice — **and he receives exactly the same conversation, in the same order, and ends it in the same place.** Neither behaviour is available as the right one. **Do not let the scene imply he should have stayed calm, and do not let it imply Liam should have shouted.**
- **The argument-shaped hole.** There is nothing in the meeting for an argument to push against: no decision-maker, no criteria, no appeal step. Brendan is doing the correct thing — marshalling evidence, escalating, asking who decides — against a process specifically constructed so that none of those verbs have an object. **Nobody says this.** It is visible in the shape of the exchange.
- **"I do the human bit. I'm the human element, or whatever" — and it must sound bad.** He arrives at the play's thesis, out loud, for the first time in the show, and it comes out as a badly-phrased thing a person says when they are losing, and he is embarrassed by it and abandons it. **Do not improve this line.** Per [[14 - The Offboarding]]'s standing note, the thesis is carried by the spreadsheet and not by anybody's mouth; Brendan gets closest to saying it and the scene punishes him for it socially, which is not the same as saying it. Compare Liam's *"is there a field for that"* — same failure, opposite temperature.
- **"It didn't." — the only warmth in either scene, and it costs nothing.** She is smoothing an awkward moment, not comforting him and not commenting on his worth. Per [[Modern World — Supporting]], Dana is never his advocate; two words of social repair is the maximum the register allows and it should land as *slightly worse* than saying nothing.
- **"Brendan." — the only time she interrupts anybody in the play.** She does it to stop him saying something about her that he cannot take back, which is a kindness, and it is also the fastest way to close the subject. Both readings are correct and the actor should not choose. One word, flat, no volume. Established as her move in [[Modern World — Supporting]]: *"when he gets an arithmetic answer wrong she says his name once and stops."*
- **He apologises to her.** The rage ends with the man who was fired saying sorry to the person who fired him, because he cannot bear having been rude. **This is the character, not a beat** — [[Brendan]] is conciliatory to a fault and reaches to collaborate mid-humiliation. It is also why he is winded and flat in [[19 - Nine Tickets]] instead of angry: he already spent it, and he is ashamed of how it went.
- **The one time he speaks up in the whole play is for himself.** He declines to speak eight times on other people's behalf and says everything here, at volume, for his own case, and it achieves nothing. **No line refers to this and no character notices it.** It is the audience's to assemble, and it is why [[20 - The Audit]] can be him furious on *Liam's* behalf without reading as a switch — he has learned, accurately, that his own voice is worth nothing.
- **"Four more like it and nobody clapped."** He says it to Dana as evidence and it buys him nothing. Six days later, in [[19 - Nine Tickets]], **Liam says the same sentence back to him as a compliment** — *"you did the portal in a weekend and everyone clapped, and then you did four more like it and nobody clapped, and that's the actual job"* — and means every word of it. **Neither man knows the other has said it.** Keep both, keep them close to identical, and do not have anybody notice.
- **The order of her next-steps block is identical; the order of the conversation is not.** Dana initiates the same sequence in both scenes — final pay and the documents, the agreement, the returns label, the access window, outplacement, the closer. What moves is only what the *other* man asks for: Liam asks about health cover and never asks about the market; Brendan never asks about health cover and drags the severance forward by panicking. **She answers whatever arrives and then returns to the same list in the same order.** That is the consistency to protect in any rewrite — not a line-by-line lockstep of the whole scene.
- **"I'll do it today." — the returns box, unprompted, against an explicit "there's no rush."** This is the one line in [[19 - Nine Tickets]] — *"I sent mine back the same day. Same afternoon, I didn't even think about it"* — staged instead of reported. He is a rule-follower and he complies before he is asked. **Do not remark on it and do not slow down for it.**
- **He hears the access line too, and does nothing with it.** *"Your access stays active until Friday… anything you need to hand over or save"* is offered to him, as policy, and he sends his equipment back that afternoon and never uses it. **The door is opened for him and he closes it, and eight days later he goes through somebody else's.** Nothing marks this. Do not let the lighting notice; the plant belongs to [[14 - The Offboarding]] and it is not being planted twice, only confirmed as ordinary.
- **No ring.** [[14 - The Offboarding]]'s tinnitus beat does not return — spending it twice would spend it. **Brendan hears every word of this meeting and it does not help him.** That is the contrast and it is free.
- **"A pamphlet." / "It's required. Everybody gets one."** The scene's only laugh and it is entirely accurate — it is a genuine statutory obligation, and she is telling the truth. See [[Performance Management — How It Actually Works]] §8. Play it straight; she is not being dry.
- **Three weeks, and the raise that never happened.** He is cut five days after a promotion whose comp change had not taken effect, so his severance is calculated on the old number. All of that is normal, all of it is defensible, and none of it is anybody's decision. Same arithmetic as Liam's *"four"* — one week per year of service, applied consistently, which is exactly the problem. **Underplay it the way Liam underplays four.**
- **Liam is not in this scene and his name is not said.** Neither is Marcus's. Brendan does not know Liam was cut and will not find out until [[19 - Nine Tickets]]. **Do not let him mention the team, compare himself to anyone, or notice that he is not the only one** — the second he does, the reduction becomes a story about who deserved it.
- **The thread is the inversion of Liam's ending.** [[14 - The Offboarding]] ends with Liam typing *"congrats!!"* into #general and sending it and meaning it. This ends with Brendan typing *"thanks everyone"* into the same thread and holding backspace. **The two scenes must use the same thread and the same forty-one reactions**, and Liam's message should be visible in it on Brendan's screen throughout. Nobody looks at it and nothing points at it. Logged in [[Themes & Motifs]].
- **What this costs [[19 - Nine Tickets]], and why it's worth it.** Brendan's *"they let me go as well"* is no longer a surprise to the house — it is now dramatic irony, and 19 becomes six minutes of watching Liam congratulate a man the audience knows was fired. That is the play's normal mode (the audience is always ahead of Liam) and the promotion/redundancy joke was already half-spent by the `AIV-076` sheet. **Liam's "…What?" still plays**, because it is his surprise, not ours. See the note added to [[19 - Nine Tickets]].
- **If it has to come down.** This scene runs very nearly as long as [[14 - The Offboarding]] itself, which is the point of it — it is the same call. The first place to look for the time is not inside this scene but in [[19 - Nine Tickets]]: with the layoff staged, the working-out of *that* Brendan was cut can compress hard and go straight to the nine. Inside this scene, cut in order: the lease, then the "Brendan on platform" line, then "at the new number or the old one." **Do not cut the read-out, "are you serious," the human-element line, "there's no appeal," "Brendan.", the apology, the pamphlet, "I'll do it today," or the backspace.**
