John petitions *lawfully* and is answered with a deeper cut and an offer that's worse than the cut. Mirror twin scene: **[[13 - The Performance Review]]** (Liam, same beat). ## Purpose
Cut John's rate — and make the audience *grant Cartwright his case before they recoil.* Cartwright respects John, is genuinely sorry, and is telling the truth: he doesn't set the price, the market does, and the old law that protected the rate is being struck from the books. His offer of factory work is meant as a real kindness. The horror is that none of it is malice and John is still being ruined — and the "market" Cartwright defers to is one he himself built by buying the looms. (Steelman per [[Writing Didactic Drama — Craft Notes]].)

The two never share the plane or touch. Cartwright is glad to see John and never once unkind; that's what makes it land.

## Script

*(CARTWRIGHT's office on the shared screen — a good coat, a warm room, the new mill through the window behind him. JOHN live, cap in hand, the folded petition held careful.)*

> [!screen] VIDEO — CARTWRIGHT · `AIV-080` *(glad to see him)*
> John. Come in, come in. How's the boy — he must be near big enough for a frame of his own.

**JOHN** *(live)*: He is, sir. *(to the matter; he lifts the petition)* The men asked me to bring you this. It's signed fair — forty of us. We're not asking for more than we had. Only to hold the rate where it was. A fair price for good work, sir, same as there's always been.

> [!screen] VIDEO — CARTWRIGHT · `AIV-080` *(kindly)*
> May I be straight with you, John — the way I would with any man I respect? I don't set that price. I wish I did. Manchester sets it, and they're selling cloth at half what yours costs me to make. Half. I've held your rate longer than any master in this valley. Ask them.

**JOHN** *(live)*: Our cloth's better than theirs, sir. Anyone can feel it's better.

> [!screen] VIDEO — CARTWRIGHT · `AIV-080` *(real regret)*
> It is. It's the best in the valley. And I can't sell "better" for twice the price — I've tried. The buyers don't feel the cloth any more, John. They read the number.

**JOHN** *(live, his faith still holding)*: There's law for it, though. A fair rate, set by the magistrates. It's been the law two hundred year—

> [!screen] VIDEO — CARTWRIGHT · `AIV-080` *(gently, informing him)*
> That law's as good as gone, John. They're striking the wage-setting off the books — this year, the next. I'd not lie to you about it. Nobody sets a wage now but the market.

*(A beat. The thing John was sure of — that fairness is owed and the law will see to it — quietly comes away in his hands.)*

> [!screen] VIDEO — CARTWRIGHT · `AIV-080` *(warm, leaning in)*
> But hear me — I'm not shutting my door on you. There's work in the new shed. Indoor, dry, a steady wage every week, no waiting on the carrier. I'd take you on tomorrow, and the boy with you. *(genuinely puzzled at John's face)* I thought you'd be glad of it.

**JOHN** *(live, quiet)*: Glad to mind the machine that's taken my trade.

> [!screen] VIDEO — CARTWRIGHT · `AIV-080`
> It's good, honest work, John.

**JOHN** *(live)*: It's half the wage — to tend the thing that's making it half.

> [!screen] VIDEO — CARTWRIGHT · `AIV-080` *(the regret real, the answer immovable)*
> It's what I have to offer. And I am sorry — truly. Take the petition back to the men; I'll read every name on it, you have my word. But I can't pay for cloth I can't sell.

*(He's warm. He's sorry. He's not going to move. JOHN folds the petition back up — careful, the way he carried it in. Behind Cartwright, through the window, the new looms keep moving. Out.)*

## Notes
- **Steelman check.** Cartwright is fond of John, sorry, and *telling the truth* — the market is real, the protective law really is being repealed, the factory offer is meant kindly. The audience should grant him all of it before they recoil (the "kind" offer is to tend the machine that replaced him, at half wage and no dignity; and the "market" is one Cartwright built by buying the looms). Never a sneer. See [[Writing Didactic Drama — Craft Notes]].
- **His flaw is the trap.** John's lie — "fairness is owed to honest work; the law will see sense" ([[John Arc]]) — is exactly what the scene dismantles. He fights the only lawful, dignified way he knows (the petition), and it's worth nothing against "the market."
- **Curdle payoff.** Pays off John's easy *"sign a petition, it gets sorted out"* from [[02 - The Loom and the Guild — John half]] — logged in [[Themes & Motifs]]. It doesn't get sorted.
- **Period accuracy.** The protective statute really was dismantled exactly here: the **wage-fixing clauses of the Statute of Artificers were repealed in 1813** (apprenticeship clauses 1814), and the 1808 minimum-wage bill was refused on laissez-faire grounds — see [[Luddites — Period & Conditions]] §3. Keep the date soft ("this year, the next") rather than pinned. Cartwright's name nods to William Cartwright of Rawfolds; keep him fictionalised.
- **Mirror twin.** Beat-for-beat with [[13 - The Performance Review]]: a good-faith plea met with a sincere, reasonable, immovable "no." Keep Cartwright and Kristina tonally matched.
- **No physical interaction.** John live (with his own petition prop); Cartwright on the shared screen; operator-cued with breathing room.
