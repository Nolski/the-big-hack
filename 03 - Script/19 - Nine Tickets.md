# Scene 19 — Nine Tickets

> [!info] Beat
> Brendan was cut in the same reduction, six days after being promoted, because his work lived in Linear and the pull was from Jira. **The audience watched it happen in [[14b - Three Weeks]]; Liam finds out here.** Liam — who came through 2001 — stops thinking about himself entirely and tries to be useful, which is the warmest he is in the play and far too late to be anything but painful. Then he refuses to return his equipment, and gives Brendan the machine. Design record: [[Rework — Brendan Goes Dark]] §C.

## Purpose
Give the audience the one thing the play has withheld for seventy minutes — **two people in the same room** — and use it to hand over the weapon.

The scene has three jobs. It lands the second layoff and lets the audience assemble the argument themselves: Liam had no column, Brendan had the wrong column, Marcus files everything and is fine. It shows Liam being *good* — practical, generous, unsentimental, genuinely helpful — for the only sustained stretch in the show. And it moves the rig from one man to the other on a shrug, with nobody noticing what has happened.

**Liam is not angry at Brendan and does not suspect him of anything.** The conflict is entirely in the house. The audience knows what Brendan wrote, knows about the promotion, and has to watch Liam be kind about both.

## Setting / Staging
**Liam's flat.** The first room in the play that is not an office, and **the only scene in the show with no screen in it** — no monitors, no projection, no operator cue, no video asset. After seventy minutes of glass, the surface goes dark and stays dark.

By the door: a **cardboard box**, taped shut, a printed courier label already stuck to it. It has not gone anywhere. On a desk, the **Mac Studio** from [[11b - Best Practices]].

Two beers. Nobody is drinking fast until they are.

> [!important] The physical contact — the play's scarcest resource, spent here
> [[Brendan]] and §B of [[Rework — The Escalation Pass]] both record that live–live touch is available and **"is being saved."** This is what it was saved for. **Liam puts the machine into Brendan's hands and that is the first time two people in this play have touched.**
>
> **Play it as nothing.** No hold, no music, no light change, no pause on either side of it — same rule as the access plant in [[14 - The Offboarding]]. Two men and a box, and neither of them notices it is the only time it has ever happened. If a draft or a production lands on it, the beat is gone.

## Live Cast

- **LIAM** — live, Speaker A. **The most alive he is in the entire play**, and it is all pointed at somebody else. Practical, fast, unsentimental, faintly bossy. He is good at this. He has done it before. Do not let the actor play grief underneath — he genuinely stops thinking about himself the moment he finds out, and that is the character's one redemption and the scene's whole engine.
- **BRENDAN** — live, Speaker B. Winded, apologetic, and trying twice to say something he cannot say. He nurses one beer for the whole scene and then doesn't.

> [!note] Live/live plane
> Both actors in the same physical space for the first and only time before the arrest. No video characters appear. **This scene needs no render, no clip, no lip-sync and no cue** — it is two people and a table.

## Script

*(Brandon and Liam are in Liam's apartment. By the door, a cardboard box, taped shut, with a courier label already printed and stuck to it. On the desk, a Mac Studio.)*

*(Two beers. They both sith, in the same room, for the first time.)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: You want a glass?

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: No, I'm good with the can.

**LIAM** *(live)*: I've got glasses. *(beat)* I'm not.... I do own glasses.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: I know you do.

*(Liam smiles)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: I just want to say... nice work on the developer portal... I know I gave you some shit for it, but it truly is impressive how quick that came togehter

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: …Thanks.

**LIAM** *(live)*: How did your call go with Kara and Dana?

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: It was kinda bullshit

*(LIAM puts his beer down... he's never heard Brendan use language like that)*

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: I've never met those people, and they were just so heartless, they have no idea what work we do, it's just... bullshit.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: You know... Marcus said they accidentally left the share settings open for the spreadsheet they were using, and there's a column on it for tickets closed. *(beat)* Out of Jira.

**LIAM** *(live)*: You don't use Jira.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: I've never used Jira. *(beat)* I asked about it. I asked Kristina whether I should move it all across, and she said work however I want.

**LIAM** *(live)*: I remember you asking...

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: She said she'd pull it in her end.

**LIAM** *(live)*: *(beat)* Did she?

**NARRATOR** *(V.O.)*: Kristina had said she would copy Brendan's tickets across from Linear herself. She built a tool to do it. Like the rest of it, she vibe coded the tool, and it never actually worked. Neither of them ever knew.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: *(beat)* I don't know.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: *(beat)* Nine.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Nine what.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: Tickets closed: nine. *(beat)* That's what Marcus said mine said.

**LIAM** *(live)*: How is marcus doing anyway?

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: Marcus is fine.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Course he is.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: *(beat)* Marcus files everything in Jira.

*(They both just reflect on how stupid the process was... It was almost better when they didn't know how the decision was made.)*

**LIAM** *(live, quietly)*: They couldn't see your actual impact

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: What?

**LIAM** *(live)*: There's no column for it. *(beat)* Whatever you actually did for three years, there's nowhere on the sheet it goes, so on the sheet you did nine things. *(beat)* That's...

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: Kinda bullshit.

**LIAM** *(live)*: *(beat)* Kinda... bullshit....

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: I don't know what to do.

*(And LIAM comes alive properly, the way he does over a hard bug, and it is all pointed at somebody else.)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: Right. Okay. First thing, polish off your resume, and update your linkedin... it may take a while to find a new job, but you can't get discouraged

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: I haven't got a resume...

**LIAM** *(live)*: You will. *(beat)* Second thing, don't tell anybody you were fired, tell them you were impacted by layoffs. It lands completely differently and I don't know why. *(beat)* Third thing, and do this tomorrow, get everything out of your head and into a document while you can still remember it about your accomplishments. You're going to speak to them in future interviews, put them on your resume and write them down so you don't forget.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: *(beat)* Okay... how do you know all this?

**LIAM** *(live)*: I was let go in April of 2000...

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: …Really?

**LIAM** *(live)*: Everyone was. *(beat)* The company called Netfold. Nobody's heard of it now, it stopped existing later that year.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: What did you do?

**LIAM** *(live)*: I fixed printers at a law firm for a year. *(beat)* Then people started hiring engineers again, and it turned out that was still a thing people needed.

*(Liam meant that as reassurance engineers will still be needed, but it didn't land that way for Brendan....)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: You're twenty-eight and you're better than I was at twenty-eight and there is nothing wrong with you. *(beat)* We'll get through it. Both of us.

*(BRENDAN doesn't say anything.)*

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: Liam.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Yeah.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: *(beat)* There's a thing I should probably... *(beat)* In the peer review. I...

**LIAM** *(live, cutting him off, kindly)*: Don't.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: No, I...

**LIAM** *(live, gently)*: Brendan. *(beat)* You don't have to do that. *(beat)* You didn't do anything.

*(Liam's focus at this point is entirely on supporting Brendan.)*

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: …Okay.

*(BRENDAN finishes his beer.)*

**BRENDAN** *(live, nodding at the box)*: Is that box your return equipment?

**LIAM** *(live)*: Yeah it's all the stuff IT asked for back

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: Did the thought occur to you if you don't send it back, they probably won't come after you for it?

**LIAM** *(live)*: Yeah...

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: *(beat)* Are you going to send it?

*(Liam is slowly remembering financially where he was at 28... he realizes small things like a laptop, a monitor, might be more meaningful to Brendan...)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: Yeah, I am.

**BRENDAN** *(live, a small laugh)*: Really?

**LIAM** *(live)*: Yeah *(beat)* but I'll tell you what *(beat)* I'm not going to send back, all the stuff they didn't ask for...

*(He points over to the super computer he expensed)*

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: *(beat)* Can I borrow it?

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: The AI workstation on your desk. I actually think a lot of the local model stuff you worked on is super cool, and now that I don't have money to spend on tokens anymore...

*(LIAM thinks about it for less than a second.)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: Of course you can.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: *(beat)* Are you sure?

**LIAM** *(live)*: Take it.

*(He unplugs it. He puts it into BRENDAN's hands.)*

*(Their hands touch.... It might be the first time either of them have made any kind of physical contact with any of their coworkers....)*

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: I'll bring it back next week.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Keep it as long as you want.

*(BRENDAN gets his jacket. At the door, holding a computer)*

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: Thanks. For... *(beat)* yeah. And honestly Liam? Fuck them.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Text me if you land any interviews. Any of them, even the rubbish ones. *(beat)* I want to know, and I wanna help you practice.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: Yeah. I will.

**LIAM** *(live)*: *(beat)* I mean it. Don't go quiet.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: *(beat)* I won't.

*(He goes.)*

*(LIAM alone. On the desk, a clean rectangle where the Studio was.)*

*(He picks up the two bottles and takes them through to the kitchen.)*

## Notes
- **The reveal is dramatic irony now, not surprise, and the scene is better for it.** [[14b - Three Weeks]] stages Brendan's layoff before this, so the house arrives already knowing — which means the first six minutes are the audience watching Liam congratulate a man they know was fired, and *"congratulations, by the way… I meant to say it properly"* is unbearable from the first word. **Liam's "…What?" still plays, because it is his surprise and not ours.** The promotion/redundancy joke was already half-spent by the `AIV-076` sheet, so almost nothing is lost. **The offsetting trim, identified and not yet made:** the working-out — the promotion, the six days, "did they say why" — can compress to about forty seconds and go straight to the nine. See [[Structure & Scene Map]].
- **Brendan is deflated here because he already spent it.** He shouted at Dana on the Tuesday, got nowhere, and apologised to her, and he is embarrassed about all of it — which is why he is winded and flat in front of Liam and why he never mentions that he argued. **Do not let a draft give him a second run at the anger in this scene**; the anger comes back once more, drunk, in [[20 - The Audit]], and it is pointed at Liam's row rather than his own.
- **The room is the event.** Two live bodies in one space, no screen anywhere, after seventy minutes of glass. **Do not add a monitor, a phone screen or a projection to this scene for any reason.** The play's entire visual grammar is switched off for six minutes, which is what makes the arrest and the hack land when it comes back on.
- **Liam stops thinking about himself the second he finds out, and never resumes.** This is the only sustained stretch of the play where he is generous, useful and good with a person, and every bit of it is aimed at Brendan. **Do not let the actor play grief under it.** He is not being brave; he has simply found a problem he knows how to work, which is the same thing that has happened to him in every scene — it is just, for once, somebody else's.
- **Do not make the advice wise.** It is specific, slightly bossy, and practical to the point of being unromantic: don't take the first offer, say "role was cut," write everything down tomorrow. It is good advice. It will not help. Nobody in the scene knows that.
- **The dot-com anecdote is thrown away and must stay that way.** A dead company nobody has heard of, six weeks fixing printers, and one flat sentence about what came after. **No meaning is attached to it and Liam does not attach any.** Per CLAUDE.md, he does not narrate his own history — he answers a question and moves on. If a draft gives it a lesson, cut the lesson.
- **"Did she?" / "I don't know." — and nobody ever finds out.** Kristina told him in [[01b - The Cursor Demo]] to send her the board and she'd pull it in her end. She meant it. **The play never says whether she did, and never returns to it.** Play both lines flat and short; Liam is not making an accusation and Brendan is not making an excuse — he is answering a factual question he has genuinely never thought about. **Do not add a third line, do not let either man draw the conclusion, and never bring Kristina back to it.** She belongs to the group of people in this play who never learn what they cost somebody.
- **His Linear use is personal, not a team migration.** He tracks *his own* work in Linear, he asked permission for it in his first month, and he was told to work however he liked. **An earlier draft had him move the whole team onto it, which contradicted Marcus filing Jira tickets on that same team** — that version is retired. It also matters that he *asked*: the deference is what makes it unbearable, and it is set up in [[01b - The Cursor Demo]].
- **The three-way is assembled by the audience, not stated.** Liam has no column, Brendan has the wrong column, Marcus files everything and is fine. **"Marcus files everything" is the last word on it from anybody.** Neither man draws the conclusion, and Liam's *"they couldn't see you"* is him describing his own row without recognising it. Do not let him get there.
- **"Don't." — Liam refuses the confession.** Brendan's third and final attempt to tell him ([[12 - Below Expectations]]: *"I want to be clear that I'm not—"*; [[13b - The Plan]]: *"the nine weeks is there now"*), and this time he is stopped by the kindness of the man he is trying to confess to. **"You didn't do anything"** is the worst sentence in the scene and Liam means it completely. **This closes the peer-feedback confession permanently — do not add a fourth attempt at it anywhere in the play.** He finishes his beer instead, and that is the seed of everything that follows. *(The backspace in [[20 - The Audit]] is a different thing and is not a fourth attempt: it is the first and only time he tries to* warn *him, and it fails the same way.)*
- **"Nah. Fuck it. I'm not giving them shit."** Liam's only swear in the show, and it should be quiet rather than defiant — a man who three weeks earlier offered to put a dongle he owned into the returns box, out of consideration for whoever opens it. **It is also the last free choice anybody in this play makes.** Play it as a shrug. It destroys everything.
- **The handover — see the staging callout.** First physical contact in the play, spent on the machine that ends both of them, marked by nothing.
- **He upgrades him.** Brendan asks for the laptop; Liam gives him the better machine, unprompted, because it is the better machine and because he wants to help. **The generosity is the mechanism.** The rig is the one Liam boasted was air-gapped and got no tool calls in [[11b - Best Practices]] — *"if I wanted to exfiltrate a codebase I'd build exactly that"* — and it now leaves the building in the arms of someone else. Logged in [[Themes & Motifs]].
- **Brendan sent his own laptop back the same afternoon.** One line, and it does more character work than a paragraph would: he is a rule-follower, he complied instantly, and he is about to do the least compliant thing anyone in this play does. Do not remark on it.
- **"Don't go quiet."** The last line of dialogue, and the play's subject said out loud, by accident, to the one person it is about to be true of — except that what Brendan does instead of going quiet is much worse. **No emphasis, no pause after it.** Logged in [[Themes & Motifs]].
- **The empty apartment is four beats at the end, not a scene.** The clean rectangle in the dust, two bottles carried to the kitchen because that is what you do, and the box still by the door. **Resist adding anything.** The old slot-19 "Empty Apartment" scene is absorbed here — a whole scene of Liam alone would be the play explaining its own ending, and this does it in twenty seconds with a man tidying up.
- **The jingle stays out of this scene.** [[Themes & Motifs]] promises the standup jingle over the empty office; per [[Rework — Brendan Goes Dark]] §E it is wanted under the counter in Scene 20. Using it twice spends it.
- **If it has to come down,** cut the "glasses" exchange and shorten the three pieces of advice to two. **The first place to look is the working-out** — with the layoff now staged in [[14b - Three Weeks]] and the Linear plant plainly in [[01b - The Cursor Demo]], establishing *that* Brendan was cut is the third time the audience is being told. **Do not cut the six days, the nine, "Marcus files everything," "Don't," the swear, the handover, or "don't go quiet."**

> [!warning] Continuity — Liam's age changed to make this scene possible
> The dot-com beat requires Liam to have been working in 2001. [[Liam]] had him at **~38**, which makes him about thirteen. He is now **~48** — consistent with *"telling me I'm hard work since I was twenty-three"* plus the twenty-five years he cites in [[14 - The Offboarding]], and with roughly thirty years at the craft.
>
> **This is an improvement, not just a patch.** A 48-year-old being aged out reads harder than a 38-year-old, the mentor gap to a 28-year-old becomes real, and "Time in role" is already a column on the reduction sheet — the play never has to say the word *ageism* for the audience to see it sitting there.
>
> **If the age is reverted**, the fix is small: move the anecdote to 2008–09 (Liam at ~20, a first job, same function) and change three lines in [[14 - The Offboarding]] back. Nothing else in the vault depends on it.
