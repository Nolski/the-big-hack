# Scene 20b — Next Week

> [!info] Beat
> Brendan keeps his promise. He brings the machine back, exactly when he said he would, and says nothing. Liam has heard about the incident and is **technically delighted by it** — he works out roughly how it was done, in front of the person who did it, on the machine sitting between them. Then he mentions he has emailed someone about a job for Brendan. Brendan leaves. Liam plugs the machine back in, because that is what you do with a machine.

## Purpose
**This is the scene where Brendan does nothing, and it is the worst thing anybody does in the play.**

He is not covering anything up. He is not lying. He is doing the ordinary decent thing — he borrowed something, he said *"I'll bring it back next week,"* and he brings it back next week. **Keeping his word is what puts the evidence in an innocent man's flat**, and the audience watches him carry it through the door.

The scene has to hold two things at once. **Liam is at his best**: recovering, following his own advice, curious about a hard problem, and still actively investing in a man twelve years younger — he has emailed someone about him, unprompted, and said he is the best engineer he has worked with in ten years. **Brendan is at his worst**, and his worst consists entirely of not saying a sentence.

> [!important] The equivalence this scene exists to land
> **Not taking credit and not taking responsibility are the same gesture**, and the play has been showing the charming version of it since [[06 - Good Instinct]] — where he quietly fixes Liam's race condition and lets Liam credit his own instinct. Modesty and cowardice are the same act performed at different stakes. **Nobody says this and nothing points at it.** The audience assembles it from a young man being pleasant for four minutes.

## Setting / Staging
**Liam's flat again.** The returns box is still by the door with the courier label on it. **The Mac Studio's clean rectangle is still on the desk**, and the scene fills it back in.

**No screen until the last beat**, same as [[19 - Nine Tickets]]. The surface stays dark while two men talk, and comes up only when Liam plugs the machine in.

> [!warning] They do not touch, and the near-miss is the point
> [[19 - Nine Tickets]] spent the play's only physical contact on Liam putting the machine into Brendan's hands. **The mirror does not complete.** Brendan sets it down on the table rather than handing it over — Liam half-reaches and there is nothing to take. **The one human touch available in this show was offered once, by the man who is going to prison, and cannot be returned.**
>
> Do not stage this as a beat. It should look like somebody putting a computer down.

## Live Cast

- **LIAM** — live, Speaker A. **His best scene and his last good day.** Functional, a bit diminished, sending things out. Genuinely, unguardedly interested in how the incident was done, and *right about it*. Warm. Still mentoring. **Do not play any foreboding.**
- **BRENDAN** — live, Speaker B. **He says almost nothing and none of it is a lie.** Every answer is true and incomplete. He is not performing innocence and he is not sweating; he is a polite young man visiting a friend, which is what makes it unbearable. **No asides, no looks to the house, no hesitation the audience could read as a confession forming.**

## Script

*(LIAM's flat. The box is still by the door. On the desk, a clean rectangle where the Studio was.)*

*(BRENDAN comes in carrying it with both arms.)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: You didn't have to lug that all the way over.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: I said next week.

*(He puts it down on the table. LIAM half-reaches to take it and there is nothing to take, so he doesn't.)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: How'd you get on with it?

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: *(beat)* Yeah. Good. Thanks.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Did you do the portfolio?

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: Started it.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Good. *(beat)* Do you want a drink? I've got — I think I've got tea.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: I'm alright.

*(LIAM sits. He is doing better than he was. There are three job applications open on a laptop that isn't the one on the table.)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: I've sent out eleven. *(beat)* Two came back the same day, which — that's not a good sign, that's an automated thing, but it's movement.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: That's good.

**LIAM** *(live)*: It's what I told you. It's the sending.

*(A beat.)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: Did you hear about— *(beat)* Obviously you heard.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: …Yeah.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Marcus has been in my DMs about it for two days. Two days. He's got a *theory*.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: *(beat)* Right.

**LIAM** *(live)*: They're saying it was an agent. Somebody's agent got loose with a token that was still good. *(beat)* It's still going, that's the thing. It's four days and it hasn't stopped.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: *(beat)* That's what they're saying.

*(And LIAM lights up. He can't help it. It's a hard problem and it's in front of him.)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: Do you know what's actually clever about it, though? *(beat)* It didn't break anything to get in. It didn't have to break anything. It just used what was there.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: …Mm.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Every single thing it did, it was *allowed* to do. That's the whole— *(beat)* There's no exploit. There's nothing to patch. You'd read the log and every line of it is a thing somebody said yes to at some point.

*(BRENDAN doesn't say anything.)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: I'd love to see the log.

*(A beat.)*

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: *(beat)* Yeah.

**LIAM** *(live, going, half to himself)*: And there's a version of that in every company on the planet and nobody has ever once gone and looked. *(beat)* I said that for four years. I put it in writing twice.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: I know you did.

**LIAM** *(live)*: *(a small laugh)* Bit late now.

*(He gets up.)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: Oh — I emailed Priya about you.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: …What?

**LIAM** *(live)*: At the London thing. She's still in. *(beat)* I said you're the best engineer I've worked with in ten years and she should talk to you before somebody else does.

*(A long beat.)*

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: You didn't have to do that.

**LIAM** *(live)*: I know. *(beat)* Let me know if she comes back to you. Even if it's a no. *(beat)* Especially if it's a no, actually, because then I'll ring her.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: *(beat)* Okay.

*(BRENDAN gets his jacket. It takes him slightly longer than it needs to.)*

**LIAM** *(live)*: You alright?

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: *(beat)* Yeah. *(beat)* Tired.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Go home. Eat something.

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: Yeah.

*(At the door.)*

**BRENDAN** *(live)*: Thanks, Liam.

**LIAM** *(live)*: Any time.

*(BRENDAN goes.)*

*(LIAM looks at the machine on the table for a moment, the way you look at something you're glad to have back.)*

*(He carries it over to the desk and sets it in the clean rectangle, which is exactly the right size.)*

*(He plugs it in.)*

*(It wakes on his wallpaper — the same ThePrimeagen meme. Everything is still on it.)*

*(He sits down and goes back to the eleventh application.)*

## Notes
- **Nothing happens in this scene and it is the hinge of the play.** Brendan returns a borrowed computer on the day he said he would. **That single decent act is what puts the evidence in Liam's flat**, and it is done in front of the audience by a young man being nothing but polite. If a production plays it as tense, the scene is gone — it must look like a normal visit right up until the machine is plugged in.
- **He never lies, and this is the last chance to check that rule.** *"Yeah. Good. Thanks." / "Started it." / "That's what they're saying." / "…Mm." / "I know you did."* **Every answer is true and incomplete.** He does not construct a cover story, he does not deflect, and he does not sweat. **Do not give him a single line that is factually false**, in this scene or any later one — the moment he lies he becomes a villain and the play loses its argument.
- **Liam is at his best here and it is his last good day.** Recovering, following his own advice, eleven applications out, curious about a hard problem, and **still investing in Brendan**. The Priya email is the knife: unprompted, generous, and *"the best engineer I've worked with in ten years."* He is putting his own name behind the person who is about to let him go to prison, and he will never know he did it.
- **"Especially if it's a no, actually, because then I'll ring her."** The most generous line Liam has in the play. Throw it away.
- **It has not stopped, and Liam says so.** *"It's four days and it hasn't stopped."* The audience has watched the counter and knows he is right. **The play is now running two clocks — a man's life, and a thing nobody is attending to** — and this is where the second one is first spoken about by a character. Per [[Rework — Brendan Goes Dark]] §D, the counter is on the surface, quietly, from here to the end.
- **Liam's admiration is the beat the old design assigned to him and it survives intact.** *"It didn't break anything to get in… every single thing it did, it was allowed to do."* He is **completely right**, he is delighted to be right, and he is describing his own indictment to the man who caused it. Per [[Liam Arc]], the craftsman's admiration arriving before the fear is the whole character. **Keep it short and genuinely excited — this is a hard problem and he loves hard problems.**
- **"I'd love to see the log."** Do not land on it. One beat, then Brendan says "Yeah," then Liam is off onto something else. **The audience does the work.**
- **Marcus is in his DMs with a theory**, offstage, and is presumably right about something and useless about everything, exactly as always. One line, no follow-up, and he never appears in this scene.
- **They do not touch — see the staging callout.** Brendan sets it down; Liam half-reaches; nothing happens. The play's one contact remains the single moment in [[19 - Nine Tickets]], given by the man who goes to prison and never returned.
- **"It takes him slightly longer than it needs to."** The only physical tell in the scene, on a jacket, and it is the closest thing to a confession he produces. **Nothing else.** No pause at the door, no unfinished sentence — [[20 - The Audit]] already spent the last backspace, and repeating the gesture here would turn a pattern into a tic.
- **He is never charged, and nothing in the play ever explains why.** The audit trail carries Liam's name, the machine is in Liam's flat, Liam has motive and Liam's own words are on the record. Brendan is invisible to the process for the same reason he was invisible to the reduction: **nothing he does generates a row anywhere.**
- **The last image is the machine going back into the rectangle it left**, which is exactly the right size, and Liam plugging it in because that is what you do with a machine. **No music, no hold on the screen, no reaction.** He goes back to a job application.
- **If it has to come down,** cut the tea and the two same-day rejections. **Do not cut the half-reach, "every single thing it did it was allowed to do," the Priya email, or the plug.**

> [!question] Open, and Movements IV–V depend on it
> **Does Liam ever say "I lent that machine to Brendan"?** He knows he did. One sentence would put an investigator in Brendan's flat.
>
> Three roads, all live: **(a)** he never thinks of it, because per [[12 - Below Expectations]] he cannot construct a world in which Brendan is the problem; **(b)** he says it once, flatly and factually, because he is literal — and it goes nowhere, because Brendan's answer is *"he lent me a computer for a few days, I gave it back,"* which is **true**, and because per the Coalfire case in [[Real Cases — Hacks, Trials & the Press]] a correct account is no defence against a room that has decided what it saw; **(c)** he says it, sees where it lands, and takes it back to protect him.
>
> **(b) is the strongest and the cruellest** — it gives Brendan his one and only line of self-preservation and every word of it is factually true. **Decide before drafting the arrest.**
