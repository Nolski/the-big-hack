# Scene 22 — Current Employer

> [!info] Beat
> They come for him politely. They mispronounce his name; they ask him to confirm his current employer and the record says he still has one; he corrects it, accurately, and it is **noted**. Then he moves toward his own desk to prove it and they put him on the floor, by the book, in about a second and a half — and then help him up and apologise and carry on with the form. Marcus is on a video call throughout and stays on it, recording. Ladder position: [[The Escalation Ladder]], Movement IV. Machinery: [[Real Cases — Hacks, Trials & the Press]] §1 and §3.

## Purpose
**This is an administrative step, not a set piece.** Per §E of [[Rework — The Escalation Pass]], the arrest stopped being the climax the moment the ending became a removal — it is the hinge, and the trial has to still feel like the destination on the way out of it. **Play it small even through the violence.**

The absurdist lever is **the paperwork, not the force**. Two officers with a form and a job to do, being decent about it, and a set of fields that do not describe the man in front of them. **The horror is that the courtesy is real and survives the takedown intact.**

And the scene has one job for later: **the record that removes him in [[24 - The Removal]] is corrected here, out loud, by him, and is not changed.** He is right one more time; it is noted; nothing happens.

## Setting / Staging
Liam's flat. **The returns box is still by the door with the courier label on it**, eleven weeks after IT sent it, and one of the officers steps around it twice.

On the desk, the Mac Studio, plugged in. On the laptop beside it, **MARCUS on a video call**, mid-sentence, and he does not hang up at any point in this scene.

> [!important] The officers are live, and this is the first time anyone touches Liam
> **Production: cast number is now four live actors** — Liam (Speaker A), Brendan (Speaker B, not in this scene), and two officers. Get that number to production before anything else in Movement IV is written.
>
> **Seventy-five minutes of this play have gone by with nobody able to touch Liam.** Everyone else has been behind glass; the single human contact in the show is Liam putting a computer into Brendan's hands in [[19 - Nine Tickets]]. **The second is this.** The touch has been withheld all night and it arrives as procedure.
>
> This pays off the hands motif already logged in [[Themes & Motifs]]: *"Liam's on the keys — skilled, then idle, then held out for a form to be signed and finally for something worse. **He never raises them.**"* He does not resist, at any point, in any way.

> [!warning] The takedown — what it is and what it is not
> **Version this assumes: live physical contact.** It needs a fight director and it is the production company's call how it's executed ([[Real Cases — Hacks, Trials & the Press]] §5); the page says only what happens.
>
> **It is fast, procedural and correct, and it lasts about a second and a half.** It is not brutality, not rage, and nobody enjoys it. He moves toward his own desk to *show them something* — to prove the record is wrong — and two trained people respond to a sudden movement exactly as they are trained to. **Then they help him up, apologise, and go back to the form.**
>
> **The trigger is his precision.** He is not resisting; he is trying to be exact. **His instinct to correct the record is what gets read as a threat**, and that is the whole play in one and a half seconds.

## Live Cast

- **LIAM** — live, Speaker A. **Bewildered, and precise about the wrong things.** He does not resist, does not shout and does not ask for a lawyer until quite late, because his instinct in the presence of a wrong record is to *correct the record.* He is polite to them and they are polite to him and it is the most frightening exchange in the play.
- **OFFICER ONE** — live. Does the talking. **Procedurally polite, and that is the whole performance** — calm, unhurried, faintly apologetic about the paperwork. Does not know why Liam is on the list and says so without evasion.
- **OFFICER TWO** — live. Has the tablet. Helpful in a clerical way; genuinely tries to fix the record and cannot. **She is the one who apologises after.**

> [!note] Planes
> Three live bodies and two screens (Marcus, and the form). **Marcus's call runs unbroken from before the knock to after Liam is gone, and nobody in the room ever acknowledges him.** He is never in the room and never can be.

## Notes
- **Play it small — this is an administrative step.** Per §E of [[Rework — The Escalation Pass]], the arrest stopped being the climax when the ending became a removal. **If the audience thinks this is the worst thing that happens, the last twenty minutes have nowhere to go.** No music, no light change, no held moment on the way out — including through the takedown.
- **Cast: four live actors now.** Liam, Brendan, and two officers. **This is the decision §E said had to be made before drafting, and it is made here.** Get the number to production before anything else in Movement IV is written.
- **The first time anybody touches Liam is the state, and it is the second touch in the play.** The only other one is him handing Brendan a computer in [[19 - Nine Tickets]]. **The contact has been withheld for seventy-five minutes and it arrives as procedure.**
- **The trigger is his precision, and that is the point.** He is not resisting. He is stepping toward his own desk to *show them the asset register*, because his response to a wrong record is to correct it. **Two trained people respond to a sudden movement exactly as trained.** One and a half seconds, by the book, nobody angry. **His exactness is what gets read as a threat.**
- **"He does not raise his hands. He does not raise his hands at any point."** The hands motif in [[Themes & Motifs]] — *skilled, then idle, then held out for a form, and finally for something worse* — lands here. **Do not let a draft or a production give him a defensive gesture.**
- **The apology is sincere and it is the worst line in the scene.** *"Sorry. You moved and we don't know you."* — accurate, reasonable, and completely true. **And Liam agrees with it**: *"No. That's fair."* He is still conceding the other side's reasoning while face-down on his own floor. **Per [[The Escalation Ladder]]: institutions detach, behaviour does not.** Neither officer is cruel and neither enjoys it.
- **"Are you hurt?" / "No."** Keep it clerical. He straightens his own shirt and nobody helps him.
- **The courtesy has to be sincere throughout.** *"You can stop me anywhere"* is Dana's line from [[14 - The Offboarding]] in a police officer's mouth and must be played as warmly as she played it.
- **"That's not something I'd be able to tell you"** is the third variant of the play's refrain, after *"I don't know that it's a who"* and *"there's nowhere on the sheet to ask."* **Liam recognises it** — hold one beat and no more.
- **The employer field is the scene, and it is the seed for [[24 - The Removal]].** The same HRIS record that flipped his rehire flag to No in [[14 - The Offboarding]] still lists him as employed, and the arrest form pre-populates off it. **Two fields on one record, contradicting each other, neither of them his.** He explains it correctly — *"it knows I don't work there and it says I work there"* — the officer helpfully types **"Corrections noted"** into a box, **and the field above it does not change.**
  - **This is the third "noted."** The calibration thread was marked **Resolved**; the judge will say *"it's noted, we'll deal with it."* Same word, three institutions, one meaning. **Do not let anyone remark on it.**
  - The mechanism is real: employer tax records feeding enforcement systems, [[Real Cases — Hacks, Trials & the Press]] §3. **Re-verify before staging.**
- **A4 stays open and this scene keeps it open.** The officers never say why he is on the list, so the beat plays identically whether or not there is a visa. **Do not add a line that decides it.**
- **The name.** *"Vass-il-EN-ko" / "Vasilenko." / "Sorry." / "It's fine. Everyone does it."* He is gracious about it, which is worse. **Surname is still a placeholder — see the flag in [[23 - The Trial]] before it gets locked.**
- **"Is there anyone you'd like us to call for you?"** The worst question in the play, asked out of pure kindness, and he thinks about it *properly* before answering. **The pause is the whole isolation beat and it costs four seconds.** Do not cut it and do not let him answer quickly.
- **He almost gets there and doesn't.** *"It's been sat on that desk"* — untrue, and he believes it, because the thought has not arrived. **Do not let him get there in this scene** and do not have him mention Brendan.
- **Brendan is not in this scene.** He is physically present and silent at the trial ([[23 - The Trial]]), where speaking is formally invited, and doing it twice halves it.
- **Marcus never hangs up, and that is the whole design.** On the call before the knock, still on it after the flat is empty. **He is right about everything** — don't open the door, ask for a warrant, don't talk without a lawyer — and every word arrives from another city, through a laptop speaker, to a man who is already opening the door. **Nobody at the door ever acknowledges him.**
  - **The footage that survives is his**, and per §D it is what the press cuts to mean the opposite. **He was recording the whole time**, which is exactly what this man would do instead of helping — and now the takedown is on it. Nothing in the scene remarks on it.
  - **He never learns he wrote the sentence that priced Liam out.** Still holding.
- **The last image.** An empty flat, a returns box that was never sent, a second clean rectangle on the desk, and a video call still connected to nobody, still talking, still recording. **No music. Let Marcus run a few seconds past comfort and go out on "Liam?"**
- **Runtime.** ~6 min. If it has to come down, cut the date-of-birth exchange and Marcus's opening rant. **Do not cut the employer field, "corrections noted," the takedown, "you moved and we don't know you," "that's not something I'd be able to tell you," the phone-call question, or "mind the step."**
