# Scene 22 — Current Employer

> [!info] Beat
> Marcus is mid-flow on a video call, explaining that what happened to Liam and Brendan is exactly what happened to the Luddites, and glossing straight past the fact that he still has a job. Liam is half-listening and doing something else. **Then the door comes in.** He reaches to close the laptop — because a colleague is shouting about Parliament and there are strangers in his home — and he is taken to the floor. **Marcus keeps talking for two beats after it starts.** Then the shouting stops and a form comes out, and a man in handcuffs is asked to confirm his current employer.

## Purpose
**This is the only physical event in the back half of the play and it has to land like one.** The removal is cut ([[Rework — The Escalation Pass]] §A3), which means nothing after this is loud: the trial is procedure, the ending is a signature. **Movement IV carries the show's last jolt and this scene is it.**

The design is a **swing between registers, not a single register.** Thirty seconds of genuine violence, then a clipboard. The same institution does both, in the same room, four minutes apart, and neither half apologises for the other. **That contrast is more theatrical than either half alone and it is the thing to protect in every draft.**

And the absurdist lever is still the paperwork. **A man is asked, in handcuffs, on his own sofa, to confirm an employer he does not have**, off a record that says he does — and the correction is *noted* and not made.

> [!important] Everyone in this room is doing their job correctly, including during the tackle
> Shouted commands are **procedure, not rage.** *"Hands! Show me your hands! Don't reach!"* is what trained people say to somebody moving toward an object during a warrant execution, and they say it loudly because that is the training. **Nobody is angry, nobody enjoys it, and nobody escalates past what the moment calls for.** Per [[The Escalation Ladder]]: institutions detach, behaviour does not. **The scene is big; the people in it are not villains.** If a draft gives an officer a sneer, one line of contempt, or a moment of pleasure, the play has lost its argument.

## Setting / Staging
Liam's flat, late morning. **The returns box is still by the door with the courier label on it.** On the desk, the Mac Studio. On the laptop, **MARCUS on a video call**, at full tilt.

> [!warning] Spectacle beat — the version this assumes
> **Live physical event.** A door coming in, four-plus bodies entering fast, and a man taken to the floor and cuffed. **This needs a fight director and it is the production company's call how it is executed** ([[Real Cases — Hacks, Trials & the Press]] §5) — the page says what happens, not how it is done safely.
>
> **Do not pre-soften this on the page.** Write the beat at full size and let production come back with what is stageable; the vault's rule is to give them something to negotiate *down* from rather than *up* to.

> [!important] Cast — this is where the number grows
> Two officers speak. **The takedown wants more bodies than that to read as real** — three or four total. The scene is written so the extra officers never speak and can be doubled, cut, or covered by sound and light if the budget says so. **Get a number to production before Scene 21 is written.**

## Live Cast

- **LIAM** — live, Speaker A. **Completely blindsided.** He is having an ordinary Tuesday and is mildly bored by his own phone call. **He does not resist at any point** — he reaches for a laptop, which is not resistance and is not read as anything else by anybody afterwards. Afterwards he is winded, compliant and precise about the wrong things, and he answers a form while cuffed.
- **OFFICER ONE** — live. Does the talking, before and after. **Procedurally polite once the room is controlled**, and loud while it isn't. Doesn't know why Liam is on the list and says so without evasion.
- **OFFICER TWO** — live. Has the tablet. Helpful in a clerical way; genuinely tries to fix the record and cannot. **She is the one who apologises.**
- **OFFICERS** — live, non-speaking. As many as production will pay for.

> [!important] The counter is on the surface for this entire scene
> Quiet, small, and never referred to by anybody. **They are arresting one man for something that is still happening while they arrest him**, and the number behind them keeps climbing through the door coming in, the takedown, the handcuffs and the form. **Nobody looks at it. Nobody in the play ever looks at it.** See [[Rework — Brendan Goes Dark]] §D.

> [!note] Planes
> Live bodies and two screens. **Marcus's call runs unbroken from before the door to after the flat is empty, and nobody in the room ever acknowledges him.** He is never in the room and never can be — and, crucially, **Liam never gets to close the laptop**, which is why the footage exists.

## Notes
- **This is the last loud thing in the play and it must be genuinely loud.** With the removal cut, nothing after it raises its voice — the trial is procedure and the ending is a signature. **Do not let a later pass quieten this back down**; an earlier draft staged it as a polite doorstep conversation with a one-and-a-half-second takedown, and it left Movement IV at under three minutes with no physical event anywhere in the second half.
- **The design is the swing, not the volume.** Thirty seconds of real violence, then a clipboard on a sofa. **The same institution does both, four minutes apart, and neither half acknowledges the other.** *"Sorry about that. You moved and we don't know you"* is the hinge and it should be delivered by a man who has completely changed gear. **Protect the contrast above everything else in the scene.**
- **Nobody is a villain, including during the takedown.** Shouted commands are procedure. *"Don't reach — hands!"* is what trained people say to somebody moving toward an object during a warrant execution, and the volume is the training. **No sneer, no contempt, no pleasure, no line that goes past what the moment calls for.** Per [[The Escalation Ladder]] and §G: this is exactly where the no-villains rule is hardest and it is not negotiable.
- **Marcus causes it, and never finds out.** The chain is exact: **he will not stop talking → Liam reaches to close the laptop → the reach is what gets him taken down → the laptop stays open → the footage exists → the press uses it.** Nothing in the scene remarks on any of it. He is right about every single thing he says and he is the proximate cause of the worst thirty seconds of another man's life.
- **The company statement (added 2026-09-09) is where the company gets its share, and it is a share of accuracy.** Malus Corp does not keep quiet. Its incident team finds one credential on every line, revokes it, and says so, and "a former employee" is what their logs say. This is the real shape ([[Warning Shot — Cotra on the Hugging Face Swarm]]): the outsider announced the attack, the lab found its own compromise days later, and nobody outside would have known at all if the thing had stayed inside. Scene 21, when written, builds the press's false picture out of this true statement. No villain: a form filled in correctly.
- **He keeps talking for two beats after the door goes**, because he is on a screen and cannot see the room. *"—for a person. For an actual person. Ever."* over four officers coming through a door. **Do not cut this and do not shorten it to one beat.**
- **"And obviously I've still got a job, I'm aware of how that sounds, I'm not..."** and he swerves straight past it into *"the ones they kept were always the ones who...."* **He is describing himself, accurately, and does not notice.** It is the tickets-and-Jira argument from [[19 - Nine Tickets]] in the mouth of the man it exonerates. **Play it fast; if he lands on it, it's gone.**
- **This is stage three of the guilt arc and the only place he comes near naming it.** Since [[14a - Checking In]] the survivor guilt has hardened into a worldview, and this is the closest it ever gets to the surface — one half-sentence, immediately converted into theory. **Protect the swerve. Do not let a rewrite finish the thought.** *"THEY'RE GOING TO HANG US ALL"* is the same mechanism at full volume: a man writing himself into a punishment he escaped. See [[Modern World — Supporting]] for the ladder.
- **"I said this. I said this was going to happen."** Said to nobody, over an open call, while a man is being cuffed — and it is **on the recording.** Per §D of [[Rework — The Escalation Pass]] this is what the press cuts: a colleague, on tape, at the scene, apparently claiming foreknowledge. **Marcus's "I want it noted that I flagged it" finally gets noted, and it damns Liam.** He never learns that either. **Do not play it as vindication.** He is terrified and grabbing for the one sentence he owns; the old direction — *"the voice of a man who has been waiting a year to be right"* — is the retired smug register and is cut. It reads as triumph *on the tape*, to strangers, which is precisely why the press can use it.
- **What Liam reaches for is a laptop, and why he reaches for it is embarrassment.** Not evidence, not a weapon, not resistance — **a small social kindness, sparing himself and Marcus a scene in front of strangers.** The last free action of his life is good manners. **Nobody in the play ever says this and the audience has to see it happen.**
- **"He shows them his hands. Both of them. Palms out, flat on the floor of his own flat."** The hands motif from [[Themes & Motifs]] — *skilled, then idle, then held out for a form, and finally for something worse* — and **"he does not raise them"** holds: shown, not raised. **Do not give him a defensive gesture at any point.**
- **"Okay. Okay."** The only thing he says on the floor. Do not add to it.
- **The employer field is still the scene's other half, and it is now better for happening in handcuffs.** The same HRIS record that flipped his rehire flag to No in [[14 - The Offboarding]] still lists him as employed; the form pre-populates off it; he explains the contradiction correctly; she types **"Corrections noted"**; the field does not change. **This is the third "noted"** — calibration was marked *Resolved*, the judge will say *"it's noted, we'll deal with it."* Same word, three institutions, one meaning. Mechanism is real ([[Real Cases — Hacks, Trials & the Press]] §3); **re-verify before staging.**
- **A4 stays out of it.** The officers never say why he is on the list, so the scene plays identically without any statement of status — which per §A4 the play never gives. **Do not add a line that decides it.**
- **"Somebody straightens his shirt for him, which is worse than nobody doing it."** In the earlier draft he did it himself. **The new version is the better cruelty** — he is cuffed and cannot, and someone is kind to him about it.
- **Brendan is not in this scene.** His silent-witness beat is at the trial, where speaking is formally invited.
- **The last image.** A door that doesn't shut, a table on its side, a returns box nobody touched, and a video call still connected to nobody, still talking, still recording. **No music. Let Marcus run a few seconds past comfort and go out on "Liam?"**
- **It should feel like the biggest scene in the second half.** If it has to come down, cut the date-of-birth exchange. **Do not cut Marcus's opening, "I've still got a job," the two beats after the door, the reach, the takedown, "you moved and we don't know you," the employer field, "that's not something I'd be able to tell you," the phone-call question, or "mind the step."**
