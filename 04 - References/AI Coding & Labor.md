# AI Coding & Labor

> [!quote] Why it matters for *The Big Hack*
> The grounding for [[Liam]]'s world and the bridge to [[Luddites — History]]. The play's argument only works if the modern parallel is *accurate and specific* — real tools, real rituals, real rhetoric — not a strawman of "AI bad."

> [!warning] Discourse, not settled fact
> This is a fast-moving, contested area (as of 2026). Treat the points below as *the terrain of the debate* to dramatise — and keep Liam's world satirically true rather than making empirical claims the play can't back. Pull specifics from current reporting when you draft.

## The honest modern parallel (the spine)
The Luddite point, updated: the question is **not** "is AI coding good or bad," it's **how AI coding agents are deployed, who captures the gains, who bears the cost, and what is owed to the people displaced or deskilled.** Liam, like a cropper, is a *highly skilled* worker whose specific mastery is being repriced toward zero — and the betrayal is social and institutional, not mechanical.

## Real texture for Liam's world (the comedy is in the accuracy)
- **The tools/rituals:** coding agents and assistants; "agent-first" / "AI-native" team mandates; PR-review-of-machine-output replacing authorship; standups, all-hands, OKRs, "velocity," calibration.
- **The rhetoric to satirise (sets up [[Themes & Motifs|the curdle]]):** "force multiplier," "10x," "this frees you up for higher-value work," "we're an AI-first company now," "augment not replace," "the future of work," "lifelong learning / reskilling." Cartwright's "improvement" sermon in a hoodie.
- **The deskilling dynamic:** value shifts from *making* to *reviewing/prompting*; senior craft judgment is reframed as a "bottleneck"; the "easy 90%" gets automated and the "hard 10%" is no longer enough of a job to keep a person (Liam's own line, in the Demo scene).
- **The betrayal mechanics:** layoffs framed as "restructuring" / "efficiency"; the kind manager who "advocates" and still signs; coworkers who adopt fastest and reframe the holdout; the silence of friends who need to keep *their* jobs.

> [!info] The mechanics live next door
> How the betrayal is actually *executed* — calibration, the dual-axis rating, the manufactured paper trail, "non-regretted attrition," and what AI adoption criteria and telemetry changed in 2025–26 — is in [[Performance Management — How It Actually Works]]. Use that note when drafting B6–B8; use this one for the argument and the parallel.

## Threads worth researching as you draft (verify current specifics)
- Tech-sector layoffs justified by AI productivity gains; the gap between hype and measured output.
- Studies on AI-assisted coding productivity (contested — some show gains, some show *slowdowns* for experienced devs on complex tasks). Good fuel for Liam being *right* and ignored.
- Deskilling / "expertise erosion" discourse; junior-pipeline collapse (fewer entry roles).
- Worker organising in tech; the [Sorry to Bother You / tech-organising](https://notesfrombelow.org/article/sorry-to-bother-you-tech-worker-organising) lineage in [[Inspiration — Tone & Form]].
