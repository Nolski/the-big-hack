# Luddites — History

> [!info] This is the quick reference
> For the **comprehensive** briefing (enclosure, colonialism & slavery, industrial capitalism, the machinery question) see **[[Luddites — Period & Conditions]]**; quotable period texts are in **[[Primary Sources — Luddite Era]]**. This note is the fast, accurate cheat-sheet.

> [!danger] Myths we do NOT use
> The play stays **historically accurate** — myths play **no part**, not even as "perception." Do not state any of these as true (full list + why in [[Luddites — Period & Conditions#Myths — do NOT stage these as true|Period & Conditions]]):
> - ❌ **"More troops sent against the Luddites than Wellington had against Napoleon."** A cherry-picked comparison; false. Say instead: *an exceptionally large domestic deployment — roughly 12,000, mostly militia and yeomanry.*
> - ❌ **A real "Ned Ludd" who smashed frames c.1779.** Ned/General Ludd was an invented figurehead — a collective fiction the workers signed their letters with. Accurate to show the *name as a banner*; never as a real person on stage.
> - ❌ **"Independent landowners fell from 250,000 to 32,000."** Dubious, poorly-sourced — cut entirely.
> - ❌ **"The Goose and the Common" as a 17th-century rhyme.** Earliest evidence is 1816 — use the rhyme, don't date it old.
> - ⚠️ **Horsfall's "ride up to my stirrups in Luddite blood"** is *attributed/traditional* — flag, don't present as a verified quote.

## The core (accurate)
- **The Luddites were not against technology as such.** They were skilled textile workers — **croppers/shearmen, framework-knitters, handloom weavers** — protesting machines used to **cut wages, deskill the trade, produce shoddier goods, and bypass apprenticeship and customary/legal regulation**. Their attacks were *targeted* (e.g. wide "cut-up" frames smashed, fair employers' frames spared), not indiscriminate. The fight was about *terms and control*, not gadgets — the play's thesis in historical form.
- The name **"General/King/Captain Ludd"** was a deliberate fiction (echoing Robin Hood) that gave a leaderless, cellular movement one voice and shielded real organisers.

## Timeline (verified)
- **11 Mar 1811** — first attack at **Arnold**, near Nottingham (framework-knitters; ~63 frames; no violence).
- **1811–1816** — movement across **Nottinghamshire/Midlands** (stocking frames), **West Riding of Yorkshire** (croppers vs. **gig mill** / **shearing frame**), and **Lancashire** (weavers vs. **power looms**).
- **27 Feb 1812** — **Byron's** maiden Lords speech opposing the Frame Breaking Bill (sympathetic primary voice; verbatim in [[Primary Sources — Luddite Era]]).
- **20 Mar 1812** — **Frame Breaking Act** makes frame-breaking a **capital felony** (softened to transportation 1814; death restored 1817).
- **11 Apr 1812** — attack on **Rawfolds Mill** (owner **William Cartwright** — the namesake nod for our mill owner); two Luddites die. **28 Apr 1812** — mill-owner **William Horsfall** assassinated.
- **Jan 1813** — **York Special Commission**: **17 men hanged** (incl. George Mellor, William Thorpe, Thomas Smith for Horsfall's murder), others transported.

## Texture for the script (people, places, things)
- **Trades:** **croppers/shearmen** (finished cloth with 50-lb hand shears — skilled, well-paid, destroyed by the shearing frame), framework-knitters/stockingers, handloom weavers.
- **Machines:** power loom, **gig mill**, shearing frame, stocking/knitting frame, **"wide frames"** making cheap **"cut-ups."**
- **Method:** secret oaths, night drilling, targeted nocturnal raids, letters signed "Ludd" from "Ned Lud's Office, Sherwood Forest," the **"Great Enoch"** sledgehammer — *"Enoch made them, Enoch shall break them"* (named for Marsden blacksmith Enoch Taylor, who made both the frames and the hammers; broadly attested — a strong [[Themes & Motifs|maker-is-breaker motif]]).
- **The state's response:** paid spies/informers, the militia and regular army, special commissions, the capital statute.

## How to use it in the play
- Make **[[John]] a composite** weaver/cropper, not a real person. Use real *texture* (the Enoch hammer, oaths, the 1812 Act, Rawfolds, the York hangings) and real *dynamics* (deskilling, wage cuts, the law siding with property) — without claiming to depict real individuals or events, and **without the myths above**.
- The **honest parallel to AI** ([[AI Coding & Labor]]): not "machines bad," but "who controls the machine, who captures the gains, who bears the cost, and what is owed to the displaced." Full argument in [[Luddites — Period & Conditions]].
