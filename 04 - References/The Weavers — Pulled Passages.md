# The Weavers — Pulled Passages

> [!quote] Why it matters for *The Big Hack*
> Gerhart Hauptmann's *The Weavers* (Die Weber, 1892) dramatises the 1844 Silesian weavers' uprising against mechanised looms — **almost literally [[John]]'s story**. Public domain: lift the period voice, the boss's contempt, the hunger, and the uprising directly.

**Source (full text):** [Project Gutenberg #9971](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/9971) · [fulltextarchive.com](https://www.fulltextarchive.com/book/The-Dramatic-Works-of-Gerhart-Hauptmann/6/) · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weavers_(play)) — **public domain** (1912 Mary Morison trans.).

## Pulled passages (verbatim)

**The boss's contempt** — Act IV
> **DREISSIGER:** "The cat-o'-nine tails is what should be taken to the beggarly pack."
- → Feeds the Wage Cut / Bailiff scenes; the contempt under Cartwright's affability and the magistrate's courtesy. Mirror for Liam's Performance Review scene.

**The hunger / grief-to-fury** — Act V (Luise)
> **LUISE:** "In rags an' dirt they lay, all the four — it didn't as much as keep 'em dry… The devil a bit you cared! You sat there prayin' and singin', and let me run about till my feet bled, tryin' to get one little drop o' skim milk."
- → The model for **Mary's** speech in the Cold Hearth scene (B9). Grief turned to fury.

**The machine named as the enemy** — Act IV (Hornig)
> **HORNIG:** "It's the factories. They're goin' to smash up the power-looms. For it's them that is ruinin' the hand-loom weaver."
- → The literal Luddite beat. Twin of the AI tool in Liam's world.

**The uprising** — (Becker; a young weaver)
> **BECKER:** "One, two, three, an' we're inside the house. Then, at it like lightnin' — bang, crack, shiver!"
> **SECOND YOUNG WEAVER:** "We'll heat 'em red hot an' stick 'em down the manufacturers' throats."
- → The heat of B10–B11. **Becker** is the namesake/spirit of John's [[Historical World — Supporting|young frame-breaker]].

**The tragic refusenik** — Act V (Old Hilse)
> **OLD HILSE:** "It's not with my will that you're here…"
> **BECKER:** "Better dead than begin the old life again." Old Hilse refuses to join the revolt and is **shot dead at his loom**.
- → A ready-made counter-character (see [[Historical World — Supporting|Old Hilse]]); the "road not taken" in [[Character Mirror Map]] — refuse, and be destroyed anyway.

**The protest song** — "The Bloody Justice" / Weavers' Song (Acts III–IV)
> Refrain: the justice dealt to weavers is *"bloody, cruel, and hateful."* (Full stanzas at the source links — lift directly.)
- → Candidate recurring **motif**: John (or the AI-video chorus) sings it; mirror it with a modern "anthem" in Liam's world (the standup jingle in the Standup/Guild scene). See [[Themes & Motifs]].
