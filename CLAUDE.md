# CLAUDE.md — The Big Hack (Obsidian vault)

This is an Obsidian vault holding the writing + production bible for **The Big Hack**, a Kiwicon talk staged as a play. Start at [[Start Here]] for the map of content.

## Formatting rules (IMPORTANT)
- **No hard line wraps.** Write each paragraph, list item, and callout line as a **single physical line** — do NOT wrap prose at ~80/100 characters. Hard wraps render as broken/ragged lines in Obsidian. Let the editor soft-wrap to the window.
- Separate paragraphs with a **blank line**, never a mid-paragraph newline.
- Keep table rows, `---` separators, headings, and code fences as-is (one line each).
- **Verse is the exception.** Poems, ballads, song lyrics, and quoted statutes keep their **intentional line breaks** — do NOT collapse them to one line. These live mainly in [[Primary Sources — Luddite Era]].

## Writing & dialogue style (IMPORTANT)
- **Keep the language simple.** Write dialogue and prose in plain, modern-readable English. Avoid obscure period idiom, dialect spelling, archaic vocabulary, and florid/"literary" phrasing. Even in John's 1812 world, period flavour comes from *content and rhythm*, not from hard words — if a line needs a footnote to parse, rewrite it simpler.
- **Show, don't narrate.** Let context and character emerge sideways through ordinary conversation and action (the model is the [[01 - Cold Open — The Standup|Liam cold open]] — a normal standup where nobody explains anything). Characters must NOT narrate themselves, announce their feelings, or deliver historical/thematic exposition in speeches. No "I am a proud worker and here is my world" monologues. Drip context in; trust the audience.
- **Don't over-write.** Short, mundane, naturalistic beats over big dramatic ones. If a line is trying to "land," cut it back. Stage directions describe in one plain line — don't novelise.
- **Live and AI-video characters can never physically interact.** The two live speakers ([[Liam]] / [[John]]) and the AI-video characters are on separate planes (like the Zoom tiles in the cold open) — no shared props, no touching, no co-presence, no handing things back and forth. Write call-and-response that survives being operator-cued against a pre-rendered clip.

## Vault conventions
- **Folders** are numbered for sort order: `00 - Production`, `01 - Characters`, `02 - Story`, `03 - Script`, `04 - References`, plus `Templates/`.
- **Frontmatter** on every note: `type` + `tags`, plus type-specific fields. Use the files in `Templates/` (Scene, Character, Reference) for new notes.
- **Linking:** use wikilinks (`[[Liam]]`, `[[Beat Sheet — Parallel Intercut]]`) liberally. Scenes (when drafted) link to the characters and AI-video assets they use.
- **Tags:** `#liam-arc` / `#john-arc`, `#scene`, `#character`, `#ai-video`, `#research`, `#theme`.
- **Scene status** progresses `stub → drafted → revised → locked` (in scene frontmatter); the master table in [[Structure & Scene Map]] reads it.

## The work
- The play runs two storylines in a **braided structure** — long movements that build to a near-climax then switch, converging at the end (see [[Structure & Scene Map]] and [[Braided Structure — Reference]]). The thematic mirror in [[Beat Sheet — Parallel Intercut]] / [[Character Mirror Map]] still governs *which* beats rhyme — keep it honest, but sequence via the Scene Map, not side-by-side.
- Tone is **darkly comic / satirical**; the comedy curdles. Log every setup/payoff in [[Themes & Motifs]].
- Two live speakers play [[Liam]] and [[John]]; everyone else is an AI-video character tracked in [[AI Video Production Tracker]].
- History/discourse claims in `04 - References/` are marked "verify before staging" — keep them accurate; flag uncertainty rather than inventing specifics.
