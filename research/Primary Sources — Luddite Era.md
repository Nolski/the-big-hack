# Primary Sources — Luddite Era

> [!quote] Why it matters for *The Big Hack*

## The Luddites in their own voice

### "Song for the Luddites" — Lord Byron, 1816 · **[full, PD]**
*(Enclosed in a letter to Thomas Moore, 24 Dec 1816; pub. 1830. Byron d. 1824 — public domain.)*
Source: [Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Song_for_the_Luddites)

> As the Liberty lads o'er the sea
> Bought their freedom, and cheaply, with blood,
> So we, boys, we
> Will die fighting, or live free,
> And down with all kings but King Ludd!
>
> When the web that we weave is complete,
> And the shuttle exchanged for the sword,
> We will fling the winding sheet
> O'er the despot at our feet,
> And dye it deep in the gore he has pour'd.
>
> Though black as his heart its hue,
> Since his veins are corrupted to mud,
> Yet this is the dew
> Which the tree shall renew
> Of Liberty, planted by Ludd!

→ A ready-made anthem for John's world (the [[Themes & Motifs|recurring song motif]]); "the shuttle exchanged for the sword" *is* the play.

### "General Ludd's Triumph" — Nottinghamshire ballad, 1812 · **[excerpt, PD — verify full]**
*(Home Office papers HO 42/119, dated 27 Jan 1812; sung to the tune "Poor Jack"; 6 stanzas.)*
Source: [Luddite Bicentenary](http://ludditebicentenary.blogspot.com/2012/01/27th-january-1812-song-general-ludds.html) · full text in **Binfield, *Writings of the Luddites***.

Well-attested lines:
> Chant no more your old rhymes about bold Robin Hood,
> His feats I but little admire;
> I will sing the Achievements of General Ludd,
> Now the Hero of Nottinghamshire.

> …his wrath is entirely confined to wide frames,
> And to those that old prices abate.

→ The Robin Hood framing and the *targeted* grievance ("wide frames… old prices abate") — proof the Luddites attacked specific machines/abuses, not technology as such ([[Luddites — Period & Conditions#4 the machinery question technology as a class question|§4]]).

### Ned Ludd threatening letter, 9 March 1812 · **[excerpt, PD — verify full]**
*("To Mr Smith, Shearing Frame Holder at Hill End, Yorkshire." In Binfield 2004, p.209.)*
Source: [Luddite Bicentenary](http://ludditebicentenary.blogspot.com/2012/03/9th-march-1812-letter-from-ned-ludd.html)

Attested fragments (original spelling):
> …if they are not taken down by the end of next Week, I will detach one of my Lieutenants with at least 300 Men to destroy them…
>
> Signed by the General of the Army of Redressers
> Ned Ludd Clerk
> Redressers for ever Amen.
> You may make this Public.

→ The voice of "General Ludd" — bureaucratic menace, an imaginary army. Stageable as a letter read aloud.

### "Ned Lud's Proclamation," 23 Dec 1811 · **[see source — PD]**
*(Home Office HO 42/118.)* Source: [National Archives — The Proclamation of Ned Ludd](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/stories/the-proclamation-of-ned-ludd/) · document pack: [Why did the Luddites protest?](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/why-did-the-luddites-protest/)
Sign-off motif to use: letters issued from **"Ned Lud's Office, Sherwood Forest."**

### "Enoch made them, Enoch shall break them" · **[saying, PD]**
The Yorkshire croppers' slogan for the "Great Enoch" sledgehammer — named for **Enoch Taylor of Marsden**, the blacksmith who made *both* the shearing frames and the hammers that smashed them. → The maker is the breaker (see [[Themes & Motifs]]).

## The law

### Frame Breaking Act 1812 — operative clause · **[excerpt, PD]**
*(Destruction of Stocking Frames, etc. Act 1812, 52 Geo. III c.16; royal assent 20 Mar 1812.)*
Source: [statutes.org.uk](https://statutes.org.uk/site/the-statutes/nineteenth-century/1812-52-geo-3-c-16-the-frame-breaking-act/) · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_of_Stocking_Frames,_etc._Act_1812)

> …shall be adjudged guilty of Felony, and shall suffer Death, as in cases of Felony without Benefit of Clergy.

→ Frame-breaking made a hanging offence. Stage as a cold, recited statute against Byron's speech.

### Byron, maiden speech, House of Lords, 27 Feb 1812 · **[excerpt, PD — verify full]**
Source: [Hansard](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/lords/1812/feb/27/frame-work-bill)

Well-attested lines:
> …nothing but absolute want could have driven a large, and once honest and industrious, body of the people into the commission of excesses so hazardous to themselves, their families, and the community.

> …men who had been nefariously deprived of the means of getting their bread… the capital crime of poverty.

> Is there not blood enough upon your penal code, that more must be poured forth to ascend to Heaven and testify against you?

> …suppose [the bill] passed; suppose one of these men… dragged into court to be tried… — Twelve Butchers for a Jury, and a Jeffreys for a Judge!

→ The sympathetic establishment voice — pair against the statute and the gallows.

## Enclosure & the commons

### John Clare, "The Mores" (and "Remembrances") · **[excerpt, PD — verify full]**
*(Clare d. 1864; works public domain.)* Source: [The Land Is Ours](https://tlio.org.uk/john-clare-and-the-tragedy-of-the-enclosures/) · full texts on Wikisource/archive.org.

> Inclosure came and trampled on the grave
> Of labour's rights and left the poor a slave…

> Fence now meets fence in owners' little bounds
> Of field and meadow large as garden grounds…

From "Remembrances":
> Inclosure like a Buonaparte let not a thing remain…

→ The single best lyrical voice of enclosure-as-loss — usable as narration or projected text in John's world.

### "The Goose and the Common" — traditional rhyme · **[full, PD — dating ⚑]**
*(Earliest traceable print 1816, re an Epping Forest enclosure bill; often called "17th-century" but that dating is unverified — use as traditional/early-19th-c.)* Source: [Mainly Norfolk](https://mainlynorfolk.info/folk/songs/thegooseandthecommon.html)

> The law locks up the man or woman
> Who steals the goose from off the common,
> But lets the greater villain loose
> Who steals the common from the goose.

*(Common variant: "They hang the man and flog the woman / That steals the goose from off the common…")*

### Thomas More, *Utopia*, 1516 — on Tudor enclosure · **[excerpt, PD]**
Source: [Project Gutenberg #2130](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2130)
> …your sheep, that were wont to be so meek and tame… now… become so great devourers, and so wild, that they eat up and swallow down the very men themselves.

## The economists & the machine

### David Ricardo, "On Machinery" (Ch. 31, *Principles*, 3rd ed. 1821) · **[verbatim, PD]**
Source: [marxists.org](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/economics/ricardo/tax/ch31.htm) · full book: [Gutenberg #33310](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33310/33310-h/33310-h.htm)

His earlier view:
> …such an application of machinery to any branch of production, as should have the effect of saving labour, was a general good…

His reversal:
> …I am convinced, that the substitution of machinery for human labour, is often very injurious to the interests of the class of labourers.

> That the opinion entertained by the labouring class, that the employment of machinery is frequently detrimental to their interests, is not founded on prejudice and error, but is conformable to the correct principles of political economy.

→ The economist conceding the Luddites had a point — the strongest primary text for the play's thesis. (⚑ He hedges elsewhere in the chapter — stage the ambivalence honestly; see [[Luddites — Period & Conditions#7 accuracy flags]].)

### Karl Marx, *Capital* Vol. I — primitive accumulation · **[excerpt, PD]**
*(Ch. 27–28; Eng. trans. public domain.)* Source: [marxists.org](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch27.htm)

> …the agricultural folk, first forcibly expropriated from the soil, driven from their homes, turned into vagabonds…

On enclosure as legalised theft:
> …a systematic theft of communal property… The parliamentary form of the robbery is that of Acts for enclosures of Commons, in other words, decrees by which the landlords grant themselves the people's land as private property…

→ Interpretation, not neutral chronicle — but the foundational framing of enclosure-as-dispossession. Good for a narrator/agitator register.

### Andrew Ure, *The Philosophy of Manufactures*, 1835 — the optimist/master's voice · **[see source, PD]**
Source: [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/philosophyofmanu00urea) · excerpt: [Fordham Sourcebook](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/mod/1835ure.asp)
The celebratory case for the factory system — the perfect **foil** voice (Cartwright / the AI-hype exec). Mine for the "machinery relieves the workman / workers better off than rural poverty" patter.
