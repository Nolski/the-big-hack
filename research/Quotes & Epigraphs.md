# Quotes & Epigraphs

Usable lines from both eras — epigraphs, projected text, chorus material, or seeds for dialogue.
**Check public-domain status / attribution before staging.** Pulled-passage detail lives in [[The Weavers — Pulled Passages]] and [[Office Space — Pulled Lines]].

## Historical (John's world)
- **"Enoch made them, Enoch shall break them."** — Luddite saying (the "Great Enoch" sledgehammer, named for blacksmith Enoch Taylor who forged both the cropping frames and the hammers). *The machine as both maker and destroyer — a perfect epigraph / motif.* See [[Luddites — History]].
- **Lord Byron**, maiden speech in the House of Lords opposing the **Frame Breaking Act**, Feb 1812 — a sympathetic establishment voice for the weavers. *Find the exact wording; public domain.*
- **The Weavers' Song / "The Bloody Justice"** (Hauptmann) — refrain that the justice dealt to weavers is *"bloody, cruel, and hateful."* → recurring chorus motif. [[The Weavers — Pulled Passages]].
- **DREISSIGER:** *"The cat-o'-nine tails is what should be taken to the beggarly pack."* — the master's contempt (Hauptmann, public domain).

## Modern (Liam's world) — write our own, in this register
- The corporate-cult banner energy: *"With every decision you make, ask yourself: is this good for the company?"* (Office Space — calibration only; write our own). [[Office Space — Pulled Lines]].
- The AI tool's on-screen catchphrase (ours): **"Want me to take it from here?"** — friendly in B1, total in B12. See [[Themes & Motifs]].
- Liam's hubris line (ours): **"They'll always need the person who does the hard part."** (the Demo scene) — the irony the whole play detonates.

## Framing candidates (the thesis, in a line)
- *"The Luddites were not wrong about the machines. They were wrong about who the law was for."* (ours — a possible closing card / programme epigraph.)
- *"Two men. Two centuries. One machine."* — tagline ([[Concept & Thesis]]).
